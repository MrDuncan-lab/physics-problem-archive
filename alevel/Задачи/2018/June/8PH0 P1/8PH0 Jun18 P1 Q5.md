> Процентная неопределённость высоты, измеренной метровой линейкой

A student carried out an experiment to determine the acceleration of free fall. The initial height of a ball bearing was measured using a metre rule.

![[8PH0-jun18-1-p5-f1.svg]]

What is the best estimate of the percentage uncertainty in the measurement of height?

**A** ±0.001%  
**B** ±0.01%  
**C** ±0.1%  
**D** ±1%

> [!answer]- Ответ
> **C**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ (acceptable answers) | Указания (additional guidance) | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|---|
> |  | **C ± 0.1 %**<br>Incorrect Answers:<br>A – the calculation has not been multiplied by 100 to give the % uncertainty i.e. $\dfrac{0.1}{93} = 0.001$<br>B – the uncertainty in mm has not been converted to cm and the calculation has not been multiplied by 100 i.e. $\dfrac{1}{93} = 0.01$<br>D – the uncertainty in mm has not been converted to cm i.e. $\dfrac{1}{93}\times100 = 1$ |  | 1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> The uncertainty for each measurement using the metre rule is 1 mm so with a measurement of 93 cm the percentage uncertainty will be (0.1/93) x 100 = 0.1 %. The majority of errors were due to not multiplying by 100 or confusion with unit conversions.
