> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Средняя кинетическая энергия молекулы пропорциональна T; среднеквадратичная скорость молекул при 25 °C

**(a)** For an ideal gas *pV* = *NkT* and *pV* = $\dfrac{1}{3}Nm\langle c^{2}\rangle$.

Use these relationships to show that the mean kinetic energy of a gas molecule is proportional to the absolute temperature. **[2]**

**(b)** The molecules in a sample of gas have a mass of $5.0\times10^{-26}$ kg.

Calculate the root-mean-square speed of gas molecules in the gas at 25 °C. **[3]**

Root-mean-square speed = \_\_\_\_\_\_

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)**
>
> Most students made a good start to the question, equating the given expressions and arriving at ½ m <c2> = 3/2 kT. Many did not go on to state that k is constant, which was required to establish proportionality for the second mark. ½ m <c2> was accepted for mean kinetic energy without comment, but students deriving an expression for <c2> needed to establish its relationship to kinetic energy, which rarely happened.
>
> The algebraic manipulation is correct and mean kinetic energy has been identified, but there is no reference to constants, so 1 mark is awarded.
>
> The correct final equation is obtained. This is restated as a proportionality expression but without stating that k is constant and losing the mean kinetic energy formula so, again, this merits one mark.
>
> 2 marks for a correct answer, identifying mean kinetic energy and stating that k is constant.
>
> **(b)**
>
> Students generally substituted the given values, converting to absolute temperature, and calculated <c2>. A surprisingly large proportion, however, stopped there and did not take the square root.
>
> There appeared to be widespread lack of awareness of what ‘root-mean-square’ means.
>
> The substitution is correct and so is the calculation, as far as it goes, but the student appears to think that <c2> is root-mean-square speed. 2 marks are awarded.
>
> Be careful when a formula uses squares to either square or square root the quantity as appropriate.
>
> Full marks for a correct calculation and answer.
