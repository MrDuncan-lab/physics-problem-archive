> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Пределы показаний вольтметра в делителе напряжения с реостатом

A student connects the circuit shown. The battery has negligible internal resistance.

![[8PH0-jun23-1-p6-f1.svg]]

The student increases the resistance of the variable resistor from 0 Ω to 40 Ω.

Determine the range of readings on the voltmeter.

Maximum reading on voltmeter =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

Minimum reading on voltmeter =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ (acceptable answers) | Указания (additional guidance) | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|---|
> |  | • When variable resistor is 0 Ω voltmeter reading = 6.0 V **(1)**<br>• Use of principle of potential divider<br>**Or** use of V= IR **(1)**<br>• When variable resistor is 40 Ω voltmeter reading = 1.2 V **(1)** | <u>Example of calculation</u><br>V = (10 Ω / 50 Ω) × 6 V = 1.2 V | 3 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> This was a simple potential divider question. It was expected that candidates would realise that when the resistance of the variable resistor was 0Ω, then the voltmeter across the fixed resistor would read all 6V. A calculation was then necessary for when the variable resistor read 40Ω.
>
> This candidate using the principles of a potential divider correctly showing clear working. The working for the 6V answer was not required.
>
> Some candidates who attempted this method calculated the potential difference across the variable resistor instead of the fixed resistor.
>
> This candidate has also answered correctly, this time by using V=IR for the whole circuit and then for the fixed resistor.
>
> Some candidates used this method but calculated the current in the circuit when the variable resistor was 0Ω, and then used this current for a further calculation once the variable resistor was 40Ω. They did not appreciate that changing the resistance also changed the current.
