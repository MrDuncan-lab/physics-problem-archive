> Поиск пластика ультразвуком: расстояние, зачем короткие импульсы, почему высокая частота

Scientists can use a pulse-echo technique with ultrasound to detect plastic in the oceans. A transducer that emits and detects ultrasound pulses is positioned just below the surface of the water.

**(a)** An ultrasound pulse was transmitted. The pulse was reflected from a piece of plastic under the surface and the pulse was detected 4.1 ms later.

Calculate the distance between the plastic and the transducer.

speed of sound in salt water = 1500 m s$^{-1}$ **[3]**

Distance =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(b)** Give a reason why the ultrasound is emitted in short pulses. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why small pieces of plastic are more likely to be detected by using ultrasound with a high frequency. **[2]**

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)**
>
> A similar question, as on previous papers, on the pulse-echo technique. This was answered well, with most candidates remembering to halve the time or double the calculated distance. A common mistake was neglecting to convert the time from ms to s.
>
> **(b)**
>
> This is so there is enough time for a pulse to be received before the next pulse is transmitted. Otherwise, the receiver cannot link the received pulse with the time that it was transmitted. A common response was with reference to interference/superposition which is not relevant here.
>
> **(c)**
>
> Most candidates made the link between a high frequency and short wavelength but rarely linked this to a greater resolution/detail.
