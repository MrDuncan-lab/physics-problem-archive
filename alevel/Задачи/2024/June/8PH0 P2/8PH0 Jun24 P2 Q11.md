> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Проверка длины волны лазера дифракционной решёткой; почему электроны на такой решётке не дифрагируют

A technician shone light from a laser through a diffraction grating. A pattern was observed on a screen 4.00 m away. The pattern consisted of 5 maxima, as shown.

![[8PH0-jun24-2-p10-f1.svg]]

![[8PH0-jun24-2-p10-f2.svg]]

The diffraction grating has 4.00 × 10$^{2}$ lines per millimetre.

**(a)** The technician wanted to check that the wavelength emitted by the laser was within the range 450 nm ± 10 nm.

She measured the distance between the two second order maxima as 3.25 m.

Deduce whether the light from the laser was within the range 450 nm ± 10 nm. **[4]**

**(b)** The electrons in an electron beam have a speed of approximately 10% of the speed of light. It is possible to demonstrate diffraction with this electron beam when the electron beam is incident on a graphite target.

If the spacing of the atoms in the graphite target were equal to the spacing of lines on the diffraction grating used by the technician, this electron beam would **not** show diffraction effects.

Explain why. Your answer should include calculations. **[4]**

> | (b) | • Use of $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p}$ **and** $p = mv$ **(1)**<br>• Calculate 10% of *c* **(1)**<br>• λ = $2.4\times10^{-11}$ m **(1)**<br>• λ ≪ than size of gap so little/no diffraction occurs **(1)** | <u>Example of calculation</u><br>$\lambda = \dfrac{6.63\times10^{-34}\ \text{J s}}{9.11\times10^{-31}\ \text{kg}\times0.1\times3.0\times10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}}$<br>$\lambda = 2.4\times10^{-11}\ \text{m}$ | 4 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)**
>
> Core practical 8. As has been observed previously, some candidates confuse d and n in the equation. On the whole, this was well answered with candidates remembering to give a conclusion at the end of their calculation.
>
> **(b)**
>
> The de Broglie wavelength of an electron is much smaller than the wavelength of light.
>
> Therefore, the gaps in the diffraction grating would be too large to show diffraction. Many candidates ignored the hint and did not add calculations to support their answer. Those who used calculations answered this question well.
