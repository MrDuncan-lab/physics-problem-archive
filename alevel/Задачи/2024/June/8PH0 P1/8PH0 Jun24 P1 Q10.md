> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Начальная скорость монеты по тормозному пути и дальность её падения со стола

A coin is given a push and moves across a table.

**(a)** The coin moves 65 cm before coming to rest. There is a mean resistive force of 2.2 × 10$^{-3}$ N on the coin as it moves.

Calculate the initial speed of the coin.

mass of coin = 8.5 g **[3]**

Initial speed =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(b)** The coin is pushed again, but with a larger force. The coin moves beyond the edge of the table and falls to the floor through a vertical height of 1.2 m.

The speed of the coin as it reaches the edge of the table is 0.40 m s$^{-1}$ .

Calculate the horizontal distance travelled by the coin as it falls to the floor. **[3]**

Horizontal distance travelled by coin =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ (acceptable answers) | Указания (additional guidance) | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | (a) | **EITHER**<br>• Use of *F* = *ma* **(1)**<br>• Use of $v^{2} = u^{2} + 2as$ **(1)**<br>• *u* = 0.58 m s$^{-1}$ **(1)**<br>**OR**<br>• Use of $\Delta W = F\Delta s$ **(1)**<br>• Use of $E_{\text{k}} = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^{2}$ **(1)**<br>• *u* = 0.58 m s$^{-1}$ **(1)** | <u>Example of Calculation</u><br>$-2.2\times10^{-3}\ \text{N} = 0.0085\ \text{kg}\times a$<br>*a* = −0.26 m s$^{-2}$<br>0 = *u*$^{2}$ + (2 × (−0.26) m s$^{-2}$ × 0.65 m)<br>*u* = 0.58 m s$^{-1}$ | 3 |
> | (b) | • Use of $s = ut + \dfrac{1}{2}at^{2}$ for vertical motion **(1)**<br>• Use of $s = ut + \dfrac{1}{2}at^{2}$ for horizontal motion **(1)**<br>• s = 0.20 m **(1)** | <u>Example of Calculation</u><br>$-1.20\ \text{m} = \dfrac{1}{2}\times(-9.81\ \text{m s}^{-2})\times t^{2}$<br>$\therefore t = \sqrt{\dfrac{-1.20\ \text{m}}{0.5\times(-9.81\ \text{m s}^{-2})}} = 0.495\ \text{s}$<br>$s = 0.40\ \text{m s}^{-1}\times0.495\ \text{s} = 0.198\ \text{m}$ | 3 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)**
>
> This question, based on a coin coming to rest due to a frictional force, could be answered by energy considerations, or by F=ma and then an equation of uniform motion. The majority of students chose the latter method. Errors commonly seen with this method included muddling up v and u and getting confused with minus values when rearranging.
>
> Those students who chose to use energy equations instead were generally more successful.
>
> **(b)**
>
> This question was a standard projectile question for a coin projected horizontally. The most common error was using horizontal and vertical values in the same equation. Candidates who had set out their working so that horizontal and vertical data and calculations were kept separate tended to avoid this error.
