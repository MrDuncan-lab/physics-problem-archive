Diodes are used in a variety of electrical circuits.

**(a)** Sketch the current-potential difference graph for a diode. **[2]**

**(b)** A package containing a light emitting diode (LED) is marked

“20 mA 3.2 V to operate normally”.

A student uses a 5 V power supply of negligible internal resistance to operate the LED.

Calculate the resistance of a protective resistor required to operate the LED normally from this power supply. **[3]**

Resistance =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)**
>
> This question examined the V-I characteristic of a diode. Most candidates knew this. A few candidates didn’t complete the graph on the negative side of the axes. A few candidates drew the characteristic for a filament lamp.
>
> This line starts to increase significantly before a positive value of p.d. This meant it couldn’t gain either mark.
>
> A breakdown negative p.d. was acceptable for MP1 as this candidate illustrates. The line could be a curve as long as it starts to increase at a definite value of positive p.d. (rather than at the origin).
>
> A very well-drawn answer for full credit.
>
> **(b)**
>
> There were various methods that candidates could adopt to solve this question. They could either find the potential difference across the resistor and then use V=IR or they could calculate the total resistance of the circuit then the LED and subtract one from the other. A few candidates used the potential dividing equation.
>
> This answer was quite common. The resistance calculated is the total resistance of the circuit and gains MP2 only.
>
> This answer finds the potential difference across the resistor then uses V=IR for full credit.
>
> This answer finds the total resistance of the circuit and subtracts the resistance of the LED for full credit.
>
> This answer finds the potential difference across the resistor (for MP1) but then uses an incorrect version of Ohm’s law.
