> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Модуль Юнга проволоки, запасённая энергия и зачем брать длинную проволоку

In an investigation to determine the Young modulus of a material in the form of a wire, a tensile force of 14 N was applied to the wire. The length of the wire was 2.0 m. The diameter of the wire was 2.5 mm. The length of the wire increased by 0.20%.

**(a)** Calculate the Young modulus of the material. **[3]**

Young modulus = \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(b)** Calculate the energy stored in the stretched wire. **[2]**

Energy stored = \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(c)** Explain why the wire chosen should be as long as possible. **[2]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ (acceptable answers) | Указания (additional guidance) | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | (a) | • Use of $\sigma = \dfrac{F}{A}$ **and** $A = \dfrac{\pi d^{2}}{4}$ **(1)**<br>• Use of $E = \dfrac{\text{stress}}{\text{strain}}$ with strain = 0.2 % **(1)**<br>• $E = 1.4\times10^{9}\ \text{Pa}$ **(1)** | <u>Example of Calculation</u><br>$\text{Stress} = \dfrac{14\ \text{N}}{\pi\times\left(\dfrac{2.5\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}}{2}\right)^{2}} = 2.85\times10^{6}\ \text{N m}^{-2}$<br>$E = \dfrac{2.85\times10^{6}\ \text{N m}^{-2}}{0.2/100} = 1.4\times10^{9}\ \text{N m}^{-2}$ | 3 |
> | (b) | • Use of $\Delta E_{el} = \dfrac{1}{2}F\Delta x$<br>**Or**<br>Use of $F = kx$ **and** $\Delta E_{el} = \dfrac{1}{2}k\Delta x^{2}$ **(1)**<br>• 0.028 J **(1)** | <u>Example of Calculation</u><br>$\Delta E_{el} = \dfrac{1}{2}\times 14\ \text{N}\times(0.002\times2.0\ \text{m}) = 0.028\ \text{J}$ | 2 |
> | (c) | • (The longer the wire) the larger the extension (for a given force) **(1)**<br>• (So) smaller <u>percentage uncertainty</u> (in measurement of extension) **(1)** |  | 2 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)**
>
> Students were able to choose and use the correct equations. However, the use of the relevant equations contained different aspects that students had to tackle. Mistakes included not converting diameter into radius to calculate the cross sectional area, and using an incorrect value for strain.
>
> **(b)**
>
> This was a 2 mark calculation most students got the correct value. Some students were not confident with determining a value for ∆x from the information found in the question. A few students successfully answered this by calculating the spring constant and using an alternative equation for the energy.
>
> **(c)**
>
> Most students realised that this was about creating the greatest extension. Some students only referred to decreasing the uncertainty in the measurement. The uncertainty is not dependent on the length but if the extension is greater the percentage uncertainty will decrease.
