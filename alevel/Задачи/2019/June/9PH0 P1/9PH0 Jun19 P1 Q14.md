> Монеты: скольжение до остановки и роль массы, косое соударение — скорость до удара и неупругость

A student carried out an experiment with coins.

**(a)** She gave a 2p coin a sharp tap, so that it slid along a horizontal surface and came to rest as shown.

![[9PH0-jun19-1-p14-f12.svg]]

The student recorded the distance *s* moved by the coin.

She then replaced the 2p coin with a 1p coin and repeated the process.

The student read that the frictional force between an object and a surface is directly proportional to the mass of the object. She suggested that, in her experiment, *u* is directly proportional to $\sqrt{s}$ and is independent of the mass of the coin.

Discuss the validity of this suggestion. **[6]**

**(b)** She arranged a collision between a 2p coin and a stationary 1p coin. She noted the directions in which the coins moved after the collision and determined their velocities.

![[9PH0-jun19-1-p15-f13.svg]]

**(i)** Show that the velocity of the 2p coin just before the collision was about 2 m s$^{-1}$.

mass of 2p coin = 7.1 g

mass of 1p coin = 3.6 g **[4]**

**(ii)** Show that the collision was inelastic. **[2]**

> This question requires candidates to consider a situation and apply their physics to agree or disagree with a statement. There are two ways to go about the problem. The easier method involved energy transfer and work done by frictional forces between the table and a coin. Both methods are published in the mark scheme.
>
> A more complicated method involved using a SUVAT equation v2 = u2 +2as.
>
> Noting that when the coin comes to halt v = 0.
>
> So u = square root (2as).
>
> This agrees with the statement if acceleration a is the same for different coins.
>
> The second part of this argument involves friction F = km.
>
> As F = km = ma the m's cancel and a is constant.
>
> A final sentence along the lines of 'the student's statement should be correct'.
