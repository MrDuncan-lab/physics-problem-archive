> Диаграмма сил для мяча в верхней точке траектории без сопротивления воздуха

A student carried out an experiment with a pendulum hung from a fixed support. The fixed support was a distance *h* above floor level as shown.

![[9PH0-oct20-3-p4-f1.svg]]

As the student was unable to measure the length of the pendulum directly, she measured the distance *d* from the bob to the floor.

**(a)** To determine the period *T* of the pendulum, the student used the following method:

- release the bob from its highest position and start a stopwatch
- stop the stopwatch when the bob reaches the same position again.

Criticise the student’s method for measuring the period. **[2]**

**(b)** The student used her data to plot a graph of *T*$^{2}$ against *d* as shown below.

![[9PH0-oct20-3-p5-f1.svg]]

Determine a value for the acceleration due to gravity *g*. **[5]**

*g* =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

> | (a) | Any **TWO** from:<br>• Should have used (a fiducial mark as) a reference point **(1)**<br>• Should have timed from the equilibrium position of the bob<br>**Or** Shouldn’t time from the maximum displaced position of the bob **(1)**<br>• Only timed one oscillation<br>**Or** should have times more than one oscillation **(1)**<br>• Should have allowed the pendulum to swing to and fro a few times before starting to time (as the first swing may be different from the others) **(1)** | Accept centre/vertical/undisplaced position for equilibrium position | 2 |
> | (b) | • Determination of gradient **(1)**<br>• Re-arrangement of $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$ **(1)**<br>• Algebra to show $T^{2} = -\dfrac{4\pi^{2}}{g}d + \dfrac{4\pi^{2}}{g}h$ **(1)**<br>• Gradient = $(-)\dfrac{4\pi^{2}}{g}$ **(1)**<br>• g = 9.6 m s$^{-2}$ [accept 9.5 → 9.7 m s$^{-2}$] **(1)** | $T^{2} = \dfrac{4\pi^{2}}{g}l$<br>$T^{2} = \dfrac{4\pi^{2}}{g}(h - d)$<br>$T^{2} = -\dfrac{4\pi^{2}}{g}d + \dfrac{4\pi^{2}}{g}h$<br><u>Example of calculation</u><br>$\text{gradient} = \dfrac{(12.1 - 9.2)\ \text{s}^{2}}{(0.00 - 0.70)\ \text{m}} = -4.1\ \text{s}^{2}\text{m}^{-1}$<br>$\therefore g = \dfrac{4\pi^{2}}{4.1\ \text{s}^{2}\text{m}^{-1}} = 9.6\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ | 5 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a)
>
> A number of possible points could be made about the student’s method, and most responses scored well on this item.
>
> (b)
>
> Although the simple pendulum should be familiar to all candidates, the method described here, in which the length of the pendulum was not measured directly was obviously unfamiliar to most candidates. Only a minority of candidates realised that the length of the pendulum could be replaced in the equation by the difference between the height of the support and distance of the bob from the floor.
>
> Despite this, most candidates realised that the gradient would be the same as if the length of the pendulum had been measured directly, and so they were able to gain credit for their response.
