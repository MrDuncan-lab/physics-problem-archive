> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Ток через амперметр при изменении ёмкости пластин во время их сближения

A teacher is explaining the differences between accuracy and precision to her students. She draws the following diagram, which shows different degrees of accuracy and precision. The circles represent targets A, B, C and D and the dots represent arrows hitting the targets.

![[9PH0-oct20-3-p2-f1.svg]]

Explain how targets A, B, C and D represent differing degrees of accuracy and precision. **[4]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ (acceptable answers) | Указания (additional guidance) | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|---|
> |  | An explanation that makes reference to the following points:<br>• High precision means a small spread of values **(1)**<br>• High accuracy means close to the true value **(1)**<br>Any **TWO** from:<br>• A is precise but not accurate (as there is a small spread but displaced from the centre of the target) **(1)**<br>• B is both accurate and precise (as there is a small spread centred on the target) **(1)**<br>• C is neither accurate nor precise (as there is a large spread displaced from the centre of the target) **(1)**<br>• D is accurate but not precise (as there is a moderate spread centred on the target) **(1)** | Credit MP1/MP2 if the explanation of high accuracy/precision is made by reference to a relevant part of the diagram. | 4 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> This question was meant to provide a straightforward introduction to the paper, focusing on the key terms precision and accuracy. When scientists make an experimental measurement they assume that some "true value" exists, and that their measurements will produce a range of values that this "true value" will fall within.
>
> Many candidates were able to identify a small spread of values as relating to a high precision, and a value close to the true value as being accurate. Some did this by defining accuracy and precision before relating these terms to the diagrams, and some expressed their understanding of the meaning of the terms through their references to the diagrams.
>
> A common mistake was to identify the degree of accuracy and precision shown in each diagram without being specific about why this was the case.
