# Question 6 (11 marks)

**Pearson Edexcel A level Physics (9PH0)** · October 2020 · Paper 3 — General and Practical Principles in Physics · вопрос 6

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Рычажные весы: условия равновесия, вес противовеса по моментам, ход стрелки

A student determined the latent heat of vaporisation of a liquid using an electrical heater to boil the liquid in a Pyrex beaker.

The apparatus used is shown below.

![[9PH0-oct20-3-p10-f1.svg]]

**(a)** She connected the heater into a circuit and took measurements of the potential difference *V* and the current *I* for the heater.

Complete the circuit diagram to show a suitable circuit. **[2]**

![[9PH0-oct20-3-p10-f2.svg]]

**(b)** The student monitored the mass of the beaker and the liquid *m* over the time *t* for which the liquid was boiling. Her results are plotted on the graph.

![[9PH0-oct20-3-p11-f1.svg]]

The student used her graph to determine a value for the latent heat of the liquid in the beaker. She concluded that the liquid was pure water.

| **Liquid** | **−1** **Latent heat of vaporisation / MJ kg** |
|---|---|
| Pure water | 2.26 |
| Weak salt water solution | 2.10 |
| Strong salt water solution | 2.00 |

Comment on the validity of the student’s conclusion.

*V* = 20.5 V

*I* = 10.5 A **[7]**

**(c)** Explain how this method might be modified to improve the accuracy of the student’s conclusion. **[2]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ (acceptable answers) | Указания (additional guidance) | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | (a) | • Series circuit with power supply, heater and ammeter. **(1)**<br>• Voltmeter connected in parallel with heater **(1)** |  | 2 |
> | (b) | • Use of $P = VI$ **(1)**<br>• Calculation of gradient **(1)**<br>• Gradient = $\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$ **(1)**<br>• Use of $\Delta E = mL$ and $P = \dfrac{\Delta E}{\Delta t}$ **(1)**<br>• *L* = 2.30 × 10$^{6}$ (J kg$^{-1}$) **(1)**<br>• Comparison of calculated value for *L* with values in table and appropriate conclusion. **(1)**<br>• But not all of the energy supplied to the liquid will be used to boil the liquid<br>**Or** thermal energy will be transferred to surroundings **(1)** | For MP2 and MP3 credit Δ*m* read from graph and used with corresponding Δ*t* value<br>For MP3 and MP4, credit $L = \dfrac{VI}{\text{gradient}}$<br>Answers in the range (2.26 – 2.34) × 10$^{6}$ J kg$^{-1}$<br><u>Example of calculation</u>:<br>$\text{grad} = \dfrac{(211\text{-}155)\times10^{-3}\ \text{kg}}{(0\text{-}600)\ \text{s}} = 9.33\times10^{-5}\ \text{kg s}^{-1}$<br>$\therefore \dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t} = 9.33\times10^{-5}\ \text{kg s}^{-1}$<br>$P = 20.5\ \text{V} \times 10.5\ \text{A} = 215\ \text{W}$<br>$\therefore L = \dfrac{215\ \text{W}}{9.33\times10^{-5}\ \text{kg s}^{-1}} = 2.30\times10^{6}\ \text{J kg}^{-1}$ | 7 |
> | (c) | • Use lagging to insulate the container from the surroundings **(1)**<br>• So that a greater proportion of the energy supplied is used to boil the liquid **(1)**<br>**Or**<br>• Use a heater with a higher electrical power **(1)**<br>• So that a shorter time is needed to boil the liquid **(1)** | Accept “to minimise heat loss to the surroundings” | 2 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a)
>
> The circuit was drawn well by most candidates. In some responses the voltmeter was placed in series with the heater, and in others the power supply was omitted.
>
> (b)
>
> This extended calculation required candidates to bring together a number of pieces of information to form a valid conclusion. It was well answered on the whole, although it was rare for candidates to fully justify their conclusion.
>
> (c)
>
> The experiment was obviously familiar to many candidates, and so many responses identified the use of lagging as a modification. Few went on to state why lagging would improve the accuracy of the student’s conclusion, as required by an “explain” question.
