> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Стоячая звуковая волна в трубке с порошком; скорость звука по расстоянию между кучками

In an experiment to determine the speed of sound in air, a powder is sprinkled over the base of a horizontal glass tube. One end of the tube is closed. A sound source is placed at the open end of the tube, as shown.

![[8PH0-oct20-2-p14-f1.svg]]

Soundwaves travel along the tube and reflect from the closed end.

**(a)** Explain why the powder forms into small piles at regular intervals along the length of the tube. **[5]**

**(b)** When the frequency of the source is 1.8 kHz the positions of six piles and the distance they cover is 0.50 m, as shown on the diagram.

Calculate a value for the speed of sound. **[3]**

Speed =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ (acceptable answers) | Указания (additional guidance) | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | (a) | • There are points along the tube where the path difference between the incident wave and reflected wave is a whole number of wavelengths **(1)**<br>• The waves meet in phase and constructive interference occurs **(1)**<br>• There are points along the tube where the path difference between the incident wave and reflected wave is an odd number of half wavelengths **(1)**<br>• the waves meet in antiphase and destructive interference occurs **(1)**<br>• the piles of powder form at the points of destructive interference **(1)** | Accept: the length of tube is equal to an odd number of a quarter wavelengths | 5 |
> | (b) | • use of $v = f\lambda$ **(1)**<br>• correct calculation of $\lambda$ **(1)**<br>• *v* = 360 m s$^{-1}$ **(1)** | <u>Example of calculation</u><br>$\dfrac{\lambda}{2} = \dfrac{0.50}{5}$<br>$\lambda$ = 0.20 m<br>$v = 1800\ \text{Hz}\times 0.20\ \text{m}$<br>*v* = 360 m s$^{-1}$ | 3 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)**
>
> This question expected candidates to use their knowledge of stationary waves in an unfamiliar context. The emphasis, different to questions on previous papers, is on the amplitude of the wave and not on how the nodes and antinodes are formed. A common mistake was thinking that the piles were formed at the antinodes.
>
> **(b)**
>
> Candidates were able to select the correct equation but mistakes occurred in not realising that the 0.5 m mentioned in the question covered 5 gaps or 2.5 wavelengths. Some thought that it covered 6 gaps, counting the number of piles and not the spacings, and so using an incorrect value for wavelength. Some thought that the distance between neighbouring piles was one wavelength indicating a lack of application of stationary waves.
