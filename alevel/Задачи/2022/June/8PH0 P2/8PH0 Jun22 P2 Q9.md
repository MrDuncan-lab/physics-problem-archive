> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Цвет лазера по картине от дифракционной решётки и измерение с меньшей погрешностью

A student used a diffraction grating to determine the wavelength of the light emitted by a laser. Light from the laser passed through the diffraction grating and the student observed a pattern on a wall 4 m away. The pattern consisted of a central maximum and 1st and 2nd order maxima as shown.

![[8PH0-jun22-2-p6-f1.svg]]

The student measured the distance between the central and a 2nd order maximum as 1350 mm. The diffraction grating had 300 slits mm$^{-1}$.

**(a)** The colours and corresponding wavelengths of light emitted by commonly used lasers are given in the table.

| **blue** | 450–490 nm |
|---|---|
| **green** | 520–560 nm |
| **red** | 635–700 nm |

Deduce the colour of the laser light the student used in this experiment. **[4]**

**(b)** Measuring the distance between the two 2nd order maxima would produce a smaller percentage uncertainty in the value of wavelength.

Give a reason why. **[1]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ (acceptable answers) | Указания (additional guidance) | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | (a) | • Use of $\tan\theta = \dfrac{x}{D}$ **(1)**<br>• Use of $d = 1/300$ **(1)**<br>• Use of $n\lambda = d\sin\theta$ **(1)**<br>• $\lambda$ = 530 (nm) with conclusion green **(1)** | <u>Example of Calculation</u><br>$d = 1/(300 \times 10^{3}\ \text{m}^{-1}) = 3.33 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}$<br>$\theta = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{1.35}{4.0} = 18.65^{\circ}$<br>$\lambda = \dfrac{3.33 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m} \times \sin 18.65^{\circ}}{2} = 5.32 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m} = 532\ \text{nm}$<br>Green | 4 |
> | (b) | • The resolution would be the same but the distance measured is greater<br>**Or**<br>The uncertainty would be the same but is divided by a greater length **(1)** |  | 1 |
