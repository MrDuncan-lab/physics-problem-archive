> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Фоторезистор: сопротивление через электроны проводимости, энергия по интенсивности, действие поляризатора

A photographer uses a light meter to measure the intensity of light entering a camera. A simple light meter consists of a light dependent resistor (LDR) connected in a circuit.

**(a)** The resistance of an LDR depends on the intensity of light incident on the LDR.

Explain how the change in resistance may be modelled in terms of conduction electrons. **[4]**

**(b)** The light meter records an intensity of 1100 W m$^{-2}$. The LDR in the light meter has a surface area of 4.0 × 10$^{-5}$ m$^{2}$.

Calculate the energy that is transferred to the LDR every 60 seconds. **[2]**

Energy =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(c)** The light incident on the camera lens is unpolarised. The photographer places a polarising filter in front of the camera lens.

Explain how the polarising filter affects the intensity of light incident on the camera lens. **[4]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ (acceptable answers) | Указания (additional guidance) | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | (a) | • Increasing light intensity increases rate of photons/energy arriving at LDR **(1)**<br>• More electrons gain enough energy to be released/free to conduct **(1)**<br>• Greater number of conduction electrons per unit volume **(1)**<br>• *I = nAvq* so current increases and resistance decreases **(1)** |  | 4 |
> | (b) | • Use of $I = \dfrac{P}{A}$ **and** $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$ **(1)**<br>• *E* = 2.6 J **(1)** | <u>Example of calculation</u><br>$P = 1100\ \text{W m}^{-2} \times 4.0\times10^{-5}\ \text{m}^{2} = 4.4\times10^{-2}\ \text{W}$<br>$E = 4.4\times10^{-2}\ \text{W} \times 60\ \text{s} = 2.64\ \text{J}$ | 2 |
> | (c) | • Unpolarised light oscillates in many planes **(1)**<br>• The light perpendicular to the plane of polarisation of the filter is absorbed<br>**Or** only light parallel to the plane of polarisation of the filter is transmitted **(1)**<br>• So polarised light oscillates in only one plane **(1)**<br>• So light intensity is reduced **(1)** | dependent upon MP2 or MP3 | 4 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)**
>
> MP1 was infrequently scored – linking light intensity to the rate of photons. It was more common for the idea of more conduction electrons to be seen. It was surprising that more students did not refer to I=nAvq given that many referred to charge carriers or charge carrier density. This significantly weakened their answers. Several incorrect responses involved confusing LDRs with the photoelectric effect.
>
> **(b)**
>
> Candidates performed well with most scoring both marks. The most common mistake was to confuse power and energy when using P=E/t.
>
> **(c)**
>
> It was clear that most candidates knew about polarisation but couldn’t explain themselves in correct language. Candidates needed to use the terms oscillations and planes, but a common mistake was to write about light in many directions with no reference to oscillations. Some failed to apply their knowledge of polarisation to the situation in the question, but instead, described a system of two polaroids (a common class demonstration).
