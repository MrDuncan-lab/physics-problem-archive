# Question 2 (6 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2019 · Paper 4 — Theory (Extended), вариант 3 · вопрос 2  
Источник: [[_pdf/2019/0625_w19_qp_43.pdf#page=4|условие, стр. 4]] · [[_pdf/2019/0625_w19_ms_43.pdf#page=4|схема оценивания, стр. 4]] · [[_pdf/2019/0625_w19_er.pdf#page=36|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

Темы: [[1.5.2 Turning effect of forces]], [[1.1 Physical quantities and measurement techniques]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Определяют момент силы и векторную величину, объясняют действие противовеса башенного крана.

**(a)** **(i)** State, in words, the equation that defines the *moment of a force*. **[2]**

**(ii)** State what is meant by the *moment of a force*. **[1]**

**(iii)** *Force* is a vector quantity.

Explain what is meant by the term *vector*. **[1]**

**(b)** Fig. 2.1 shows a tower crane used to lift a load on a construction site.

![[0625-w19-43-p4-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 2.1**

Explain how the counterweight prevents the crane from toppling over. **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(a)(i)** moment = force × perpendicular distance
>
> **(b)** total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment OR resultant turning effect = 0

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | moment = force × distance | C1 |
> |  | moment = force × perpendicular distance | A1 |
> | (a)(ii) | turning effect owtte | B1 |
> | (a)(iii) | <u>(quantity that has) magnitude and direction</u> | B1 |
> | (b) | provides (anticlockwise) moment | M1 |
> |  | total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment OR resultant turning effect = 0 | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a) (i)** Good answers followed the instruction to write an equation in words. Incomplete answers only referred to force times distance and did not note that the distance must be a perpendicular distance. Some candidates confused moment with momentum.
>
> **(ii)** Stronger candidates referred to the turning effect of a force. Many candidates gave a vague answer and stated that the moment of a force was a turning force.
>
> **(iii)** There were many good answers here stating that a vector was a quantity with both magnitude and direction. Some candidates omitted to include that a vector had magnitude.
>
> **(b)** Stronger candidates stated that the counterweight provided a moment and that it was equal and opposite to the moment provided by the load. Some candidates just referred to unbalanced or balanced forces.
