> Мощность резисторов и потери при передаче электроэнергии.

**(a)** Fig. 8.1 shows two resistors **X** and **Y** in series.

![[0625-w14-32-p12-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 8.1**

Complete the table below, using only the symbols *I* and *R*, alone or in combination.

| resistor | resistance | current | potential difference | power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **X** | *R* | *I* |  | *I*$^{2}$*R* |
| **Y** | 2*R* |  | 2*IR* |  |

**[3]**

**(b)** Fig. 8.2 represents the system used to transmit electricity from a power station to a factory.

![[0625-w14-32-p12-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 8.2**

The power station generates 11 000 V and supplies a current of 750 A. The total resistance of the power lines between the power station and the factory is 1.5 Ω.

Calculate

**(i)** the power output of the power station,

power =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(ii)** the potential difference across the 1.5 Ω of the power lines,

potential difference =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(iii)** the power supplied to the factory.

power =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[3]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)(iii)** (power supplied to factory =) 9875 × 750
>
> **(b)(iii)** 7.4 × 10$^{6}$ W **OR** 7400 kW

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | one mark for each correct entry in table:<br>row **X**: potential difference $IR$<br>row **Y**: current $I$; power $2I^{2}R$ | B3 |
> | (b)(i) | ($P = IV$ = 750 × 11000 =) 8.3 × 10$^{6}$ W (8300 kW) | B1 |
> | (b)(ii) | ($V = IR$ = 750 × 1.5 =) 1100 V | B1 |
> | (b)(iii) | (voltage to factory = 11 000 – 1125 =) 9875 V | C1 |
> |  | (power supplied to factory =) 9875 × 750 | A1 |
> |  | 7.4 × 10$^{6}$ W **OR** 7400 kW | A1 |
> |  | **OR** |  |
> |  | <u>power loss in cables</u> = $I^{2}R$ **OR** 750$^{2}$ × 1.5 | (C1) |
> |  | (=) 8.44 × 10$^{5}$ (W) | (A1) |
> |  | (power to factory = 8.25 × 10$^{6}$ – 8.44 × 10$^{5}$ =) 7.4 × 10$^{6}$ W **OR** 7400 kW | (A1) |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Many responses gained full credit on this part although some candidates did not understand that the current is the same everywhere in a series circuit so 2*I* was often seen in the box for current.
>
> **(b) (i)(ii)** These were generally well answered, most errors being incorrect substitution due to not
>
> understanding what the quantities really meant.
>
> **(iii)** Only the highest achieving candidates correctly determined the power supply to the factory. Many candidates simply calculated without explanation 8.4 x 10$^{5}$ W, the power loss in the cables, a valid step towards calculating the power supply, possibly thinking that this was the final answer.
