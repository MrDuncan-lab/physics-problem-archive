# Question 3 (7 marks)

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Силы сопротивления и тяги при равномерном движении поезда.

A train is passing through a station at constant speed, as shown in Fig. 3.1. The track is horizontal.

![[0625-s12-21-p6-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 3.1**

The engine produces a forward thrust of 70 000 N. There is a 25 000 N force opposing the motion, due to friction in the wheels.

**(a)** Mark these forces on Fig. 3.1, using an arrow labelled 70 000 N and an arrow labelled 25 000 N. **[2]**

**(b)** The train is travelling at constant speed, so there must be another horizontal force acting on it.

**(i)** State the direction of this force.

**(ii)** Calculate the size of this force.

size of force =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ N

**(iii)** Suggest what might be causing this force. **[3]**

**(c)** Once the train has passed the station, the driver increases the engine’s forward thrust.

All other forces stay the same.

**(i)** What happens to the train?  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(ii)** Why does this happen?  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(c)(ii)** idea of unbalanced force e.g. forward force \> backward force NOT just forward force is bigger

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | 70 000 (N) arrow to right accept labelled “thrust” | B1 |
> |  | 25 000 (N) arrow to left accept labelled “friction” | B1 |
> | (b)(i) | to left OR backward OR opposing motion | B1 |
> | (b)(ii) | 45 000 (N) | B1 |
> | (b)(iii) | air friction/air resistance/drag NOT wind/wheels/weight<br>NOT if any incorrect extra e.g. weight | B1 |
> | (c)(i) | accelerates OR speed increases OR moves faster | M1 |
> | (c)(ii) | idea of unbalanced force e.g. forward force \> backward force<br>NOT just forward force is bigger | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** The convention, whereby force arrows start on the object where they are acting, appears to be infrequently taught, because it was rarely seen. This was, however, not penalised. Most candidates were able to score full credit, although there were some who showed one or more of the arrows in a vertical direction.

>
> **(b)** Candidates were able to give the direction of the force and (usually) its size, but were far less sure about the cause of this force.
>
> **(c)** Most realised that the train’s speed would increase, but only the more able candidates realised anything about the forces now being unbalanced.
