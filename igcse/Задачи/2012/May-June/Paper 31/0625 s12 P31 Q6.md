# Question 6 (6 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2012 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 1 · вопрос 6

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Нагрев и расширение колбы и воды в стеклянном сосуде.

Fig. 6.1 shows a glass flask full of water at 10 °C and sealed with a bung. A long glass tube passes through the bung into the water. The water level in the tube is at X.

![[0625-s12-31-p7-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 6.1**

When the flask is placed in hot water, the water level initially falls a little below X, and then rises some way above X.

**(a)** Suggest why

**(i)** the water level initially falls, **[2]**

**(ii)** the water level then rises, **[2]**

**(iii)** the rise is greater than the fall. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest a change to the apparatus that would make the fall and rise of the water level greater. **[1]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | Glass / flask receives heat / rises in temperature | B1 |
> |  | Glass / flask expands | B1 |
> | (a)(ii) | Heat flows through glass to water OR Water receives heat / thermal energy<br><u>from / conducted by glass OR Water temperature rises OR Water molecules</u><br>move faster / gain K.E. | B1 |
> |  | Water expands / Water molecules move further apart | B1 |
> | (a)(iii) | Glass / solid expands less OR water / liquid expands more | B1 |
> | (b) | Use a bigger flask OR a narrower tube<br><u>OR Use a solid and a liquid that expand more</u> | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> This was a question in which candidates were expected to apply their knowledge to an unfamiliar situation. In many cases, candidates did not realise that the general theme of the question was the expansion of solids and liquids.
>
> **(a) (i)** The initial fall of the water in the tube was frequently explained in terms of evaporation of water or change of pressure of the water. Few grasped the idea that the glass absorbed heat and expanded first, for which full credit could be given.
>
> **(ii)** This part was better answered, with candidates correctly realising that the heating of the water and its expansion was responsible for the rise. Following incorrect responses in **(i)**, however, many candidates here continued with the same ideas and therefore could not be awarded credit.
>
> **(iii)** Few candidates, including some who had gained full credit so far, now suggested that water expands more than glass.
>
> **(b)** The most common correct answer was that of making the glass tube narrower. Many of the alternative suggestions made were unclear or incorrect, such as making the glass thinner, or making the tube longer.
