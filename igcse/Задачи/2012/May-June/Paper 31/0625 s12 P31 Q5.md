# Question 5 (9 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2012 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 1 · вопрос 5  
Источник: [[_pdf/2012/0625_s12_qp_31.pdf#page=6|условие, стр. 6]] · [[_pdf/2012/0625_s12_ms_31.pdf#page=6|схема оценивания, стр. 6]] · [[_pdf/2012/0625_s12_er.pdf#page=19|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 s12 P31]]  
Темы: [[2.2.2 Specific heat capacity]], [[2.2.3 Melting, boiling and evaporation]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Теплоёмкость воды и теплота плавления льда.

**(a)** Suggest

**(i)** an example of a change of state resulting from the removal of thermal energy from a quantity of material, **[1]**

**(ii)** the effect of this change of state on the temperature of the material. **[1]**

**(b)** Define the *thermal capacity* of a body. **[2]**

**(c)** A polystyrene cup holds 250 g of water at 20 °C. In order to cool the water to make a cold drink, small pieces of ice at 0 °C are added until the water reaches 0 °C and no unmelted ice is present.

\[specific heat capacity of water = 4.2 J / (g °C), specific latent heat of fusion of ice = 330 J / g\]

Assume no thermal energy is lost or gained by the cup.

**(i)** Calculate the thermal energy lost by the water in cooling to 0 °C.

thermal energy lost =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(ii)** State the thermal energy gained by the ice in melting.

thermal energy gained =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(iii)** Calculate the mass of ice added.

mass of ice =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(c)(i)** = 21000 J
>
> **(c)(iii)** = 63.6 g at least 2 s.f.

> | (a)(i) | e.g. freezing, solidification, condensation<br>OR example e.g. water to ice, steam to water, gas to solid | B1 |

> | (b) | Heat/energy required to change temperature of the body | B1 |

> |  | OR<br>mass (of body) × specific heat capacity | (B2) |

> | (c)(iii) | Q = mL OR m = Q/L OR either in words<br>OR 21000 = m × 330 OR m = 21000/330 | C1 |
> |  | = 63.6 g at least 2 s.f. | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a) (i)** Various examples of changes of state were accepted, as long as they were equivalent to gas to liquid, or liquid to solid, or an example of either. Evaporation was accepted as long as the answer in **(ii)** was ‘fall in temperature’ or ‘cools down’.
>
> **(ii)** A significant number of candidates suggested a fall in temperature, perhaps on the basis that the material cools before the change of state.
>
> **(b)** Some answers gave a definition of specific heat capacity instead of thermal capacity, for which credit could not be awarded.
>
> **(c) (i)** Most candidates stated and used the formula correctly.
>
> **(ii)** Fewer candidates than expected realised that the thermal energy gained by the ice was the same as the thermal energy lost by the water.
>
> **(iii)** Candidates who used 21 000 J or the incorrect value from **(ii)**, and then used *H* = *mL* correctly, were awarded full credit.
