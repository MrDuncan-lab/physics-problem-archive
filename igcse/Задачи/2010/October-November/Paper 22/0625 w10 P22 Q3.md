# Question 3 (6 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2010 · Paper 2 — Core theory, вариант 2 · вопрос 3  
Источник: [[_pdf/2010/0625_w10_qp_22.pdf#page=6|условие, стр. 6]] · [[_pdf/2010/0625_w10_ms_22.pdf#page=3|схема оценивания, стр. 3]] · [[_pdf/2010/0625_w10_er.pdf#page=10|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Равновесие сил самолёта и средняя скорость полёта.

**(a)** An aeroplane is flying horizontally at a steady speed in a straight line.

Fig. 3.1 shows three of the four forces acting on it.

![[0625-w10-22-p6-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 3.1**

**(i)** In order to fly horizontally at a steady speed, which two of the forces shown on the aeroplane **must** be equal?

\_\_\_\_\_\_ and  \_\_\_\_\_\_ are equal. **[1]**

**(ii)** In order to fly horizontally in a straight line, there must be a fourth force acting on the plane.

Draw an arrow on Fig. 3.1 to represent this force. **[1]**

**(b)** The aeroplane in Fig. 3.1 flies an outward journey from Budapest (Hungary) to Palermo (Italy) in 2.75 hours. The distance is 2200 km.

**(i)** Calculate, in km / h, the average speed of the aeroplane.

average speed =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ km / h **[3]**

**(ii)** On the return journey from Palermo to Budapest, the journey time is shorter, even though the engine thrust is the same.

Suggest what might have caused the return journey to be shorter. **[1]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)(i)** 800 (km/h)

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | (engine) thrust and (air) friction | B1 |
> | (a)(ii) | force shown vertically upwards, anywhere on plane | B1 |
> | (b)(i) | v = s/t in any form | C1 |
> |  | 2200/2.75 | C1 |
> |  | 800 (km/h) | A1 |
> | (b)(ii) | idea of<br>headwind on outward journey<br>OR tailwind on return journey<br>OR shorter route on return journey<br>OR air friction is less<br>OR idea of less weight<br>NOT flies slower | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** This part was well done, showing good understanding.
>
> **(b)** This was also well done, except by those who thought distance = speed/time. A good proportion of candidates made an intelligent attempt at **(ii)**, usually along the lines of friction being less or having a tailwind.
>
> \[800 km/hr\]
