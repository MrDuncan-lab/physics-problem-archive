> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Скорость подъёма фуникулёра, энергия и полезная мощность двигателя.

Fig. 5.1 shows a model cable-car system. It is driven by an electric motor coupled to a gear system.

![[0625-w10-31-p8-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 5.1**

The model cable-car has a mass of 5.0 kg and is lifted from the bottom pulley to the top pulley in 40 s. It stops automatically at the top.

**(a)** Calculate

**(i)** the average speed of the cable-car,

average speed =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(ii)** the gravitational potential energy gained by the cable-car,

gravitational potential energy gained =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(iii)** the useful output power of the driving mechanism.

power =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(b)** How would the electrical power input to the motor compare with your answer to **(a)(iii)**? **[1]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(a)(i)** 0.15 m/s or 15 cm/s (if answer only, 1 mark for either if no units)
>
> **(a)(ii)** 100 J OR 98.1 J OR 98 J
>
> **(a)(iii)** 2.5 W OR 2.45 W e.c.f. from (ii)

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a) (i)** It was rare to see wrong answers for this part, and even these usually gained a mark for the correct formula.
>
> \[0.15 m/s\]
>
> **(ii)** Most candidates quoted the formula for gravitational potential energy and proceeded to the correct numerical answer and unit. Use of g = 9.8 or 9.81 N/kg was allowed on this occasion, although candidates are expected to use 10 N/kg as quoted on the front of the question paper.
>
> \[100 J\]
>
> **(iii)** Many candidates failed to link this calculation to the previous one. They often wrote down P = W/t and used this wrongly, presumably not equating work with the increase in potential energy.
>
> \[2.5 W\]
>
> **(iv)** Many correct statements were offered, clearly showing understanding of efficiency.
