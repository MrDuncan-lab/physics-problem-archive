# Question 7 (5 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2010 · Paper 2 — Core theory, вариант 1 · вопрос 7  
Источник: [[_pdf/2010/0625_w10_qp_21.pdf#page=9|условие, стр. 9]] · [[_pdf/2010/0625_w10_ms_21.pdf#page=4|схема оценивания, стр. 4]] · [[_pdf/2010/0625_w10_er.pdf#page=7|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 w10 P21]]  
Темы: [[3.2.2 Refraction of light]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Построение преломлённого луча и полное внутреннее отражение.

**(a)** In Fig. 7.1, a ray of light is shown passing into water from air. The angle of the refracted ray to the normal is 40°.

On Fig. 7.1, mark clearly the angle of incidence *i*. **[1]**

![[0625-w10-21-p9-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 7.1**

**(b)** In Fig. 7.2, a ray of light is shown in water and reaching the surface with the air at an angle of 40° to the normal.

![[0625-w10-21-p9-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 7.2**

**(i)** On Fig. 7.2, draw accurately the path of the ray in the air. **[2]**

**(ii)** The angle in the water in Fig. 7.2 is increased from 40° to 70°, and the ray no longer emerges into the air.

State what happens to the ray at the surface and explain why this happens. **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)(i)** angle same as in Fig. 7.1, by eye
>
> **(b)(ii)** critical angle exceeded

> | (a) | i correctly shown | B1 |
> | (b)(i) | ray shown in air at angle \> 40° | C1 |
> |  | angle same as in Fig. 7.1, by eye | A1 |
> | (b)(ii) | ray reflected (MO if says along surface) | M1 |
> |  | critical angle exceeded | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** A large proportion of candidates could not mark the angle of incidence correctly. The angle with the surface was a common mistake, but other angles too were marked by some.
>
> **(b) (i)** Most showed the ray going in something like the correct direction, but the second mark was for accuracy, as indicated by the wording of the question, and fewer scored this mark. Of the incorrect attempts, refraction towards the normal was popular, as was reflection off the normal.
>
> **(ii)** Few candidates showed a secure understanding of total internal reflection.
