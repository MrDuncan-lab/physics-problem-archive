# Question 8 (9 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2010 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 2 · вопрос 8

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Электризация стержня и индукция зарядов на проводящем шаре.

**(a)** State the law of attraction and repulsion between electrostatic charges. **[2]**

**(b)** Sometimes, when people have been riding in a car, they get an electric shock from the door handle as they get out of the car.

Suggest why this happens. **[2]**

**(c)** A plastic rod is rubbed with a cloth and becomes positively charged. After charging, the rod is held close to the suspended table-tennis ball shown in Fig. 8.1. The table-tennis ball is covered with metal paint and is initially uncharged.

![[0625-s10-32-p14-f1.png]]

**Fig. 8.1**

**(i)** Describe what happens to the charges on the metal-painted table-tennis ball as the positively-charged rod is brought close to the ball. **[1]**

**(ii)** The ball is attracted towards the charged rod.

Explain why this happens. **[2]**

**(iii)** When it is a few centimetres away from the rod, the ball is briefly touched by a wire connected to earth.

In terms of the movement of charges, describe what happens to the charge on the ball. **[2]**

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** The law of attraction was generally well known.
>
> **(b)** Many candidates were not able to explain the situation convincingly. They often stated that the “shock” was repulsion between like charges on the hand and the handle rather than the flow of charge through the person. Full marks were given whether the candidates gave an explanation in terms of the person becoming charged or the car.
>
> **(c)** Candidates did not always appear to understand the parts of this question adequately or failed to think through the logic and Physics of their answers.
>
> **(i)** The question asks for a description of what happens to the charge on the ball. Instead of describing the movement of electrons, or negative charges, many answered merely that “unlike charges attract”.
>
> **(ii)** Most candidates scored the first mark for explaining how negative charges on the ball are attracted to the rod. Very few scored the second mark usually by explaining that this force was larger than the force of repulsion between the rod and the positive charge on the ball. A significant minority thought that there was transfer of charge between the rod and ball.
>
> **(iii)** It was encouraging to see here a basic understanding of induction, with many candidates recognising that the ball becomes negative. Unfortunately some candidates suggested that the ball became neutral. Comparatively few candidates explained that negative charges, or better still electrons, flow from the earth to the rod in the process described.
