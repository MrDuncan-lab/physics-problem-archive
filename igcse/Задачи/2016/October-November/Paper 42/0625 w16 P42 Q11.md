# Question 11 (4 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2016 · Paper 4 — Theory (Extended), вариант 2 · вопрос 11  
Источник: [[_pdf/2016/0625_w16_qp_42.pdf#page=20|условие, стр. 20]] · [[_pdf/2016/0625_w16_ms_42.pdf#page=9|схема оценивания, стр. 9]] · [[_pdf/2016/0625_w16_er.pdf#page=28|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 w16 P42]]  
Темы: [[5.2.4 Half-life]], [[5.2.1 Detection of radioactivity]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Вычислить оставшееся число радиоактивных атомов и показания детектора после нескольких периодов полураспада с учётом фона.

A radioactive nuclide has a half-life of 4.0 days. A sample contains 9.6 × 10$^{8}$ atoms of the nuclide.

**(a)** Calculate the number of atoms of the nuclide remaining after 12 days.

number =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(b)** The count rate from the sample is measured in a laboratory where the background count rate is 16 counts / minute.

A detector is placed at a fixed distance from the sample. The initial count rate measured by the detector is 160 counts / minute.

Calculate the count rate measured by the detector after 12 days.

count rate =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(a)** 1.2 × 10$^{8}$(atoms)
>
> **(b)** (144 / 8 + 16 = 18 + 16 =) 34 counts / minute

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | 9.6 × 10$^{8}$ / 8 | C1 |
> |  | 1.2 × 10$^{8}$(atoms) | A1 |
> | (b) | 160 – 16 OR 144 | C1 |
> |  | (144 / 8 + 16 = 18 + 16 =) 34 counts / minute | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Most candidates answered this question well. Weaker candidates struggled to recognise that 3 half-lives was available from the data. Other common errors were to calculate the number of atoms after four half-lives, or having realised that 12 days was equivalent to three half-lives, to merely divide the number of atoms in the sample by three.
>
> **(b)** Only the strongest candidates gained full credit. Many candidates recognised the need to subtract the background count of 16 at the start of the answer but some did not add the background count of 16 back on after correctly dividing 144 by 8 so 18 was often seen as the final answer.
