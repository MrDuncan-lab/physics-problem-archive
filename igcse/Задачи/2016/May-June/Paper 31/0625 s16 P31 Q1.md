# Question 1 (10 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2016 · Paper 3 — Theory (Core), вариант 1 · вопрос 1  
Источник: [[_pdf/2016/0625_s16_qp_31.pdf#page=2|условие, стр. 2–3]] · [[_pdf/2016/0625_s16_ms_31.pdf#page=4|схема оценивания, стр. 4]] · [[_pdf/2016/0625_s16_er.pdf#page=15|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 s16 P31]]  
Темы: [[1.2 Motion]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Сравнить движение велосипедиста и бегуна по графикам скорости, найти путь и достроить график торможения.

Fig. 1.1 shows part of the speed-time graphs for a cyclist and for a runner.

![[0625-s16-31-p2-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 1.1**

**(a)** Compare the motion of the cyclist and the runner during the first 6 seconds. Explain your answer. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe the motion of the cyclist between time *t* = 6.0 s and time *t* = 12.0 s. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the total distance travelled by the cyclist between *t* = 0 and *t* = 12.0 s.

distance travelled =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ m **[4]**

**(d)** After the first 6.0 seconds, the runner moves at constant speed for 4.0 seconds. He then slows down uniformly and stops in a further 2.0 seconds.

On Fig. 1.1, complete the graph for the runner’s motion. **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(c)** <u>81(m)</u>

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | cyclist accelerating OR moving faster OR cyclist has higher speed | B1 |
> |  | both (cyclist and runner) accelerating | B1 |
> |  | cyclists gradient steeper OR acceleration values calculated | B1 |
> | (b) | Constant OR steady OR uniform (speed or motion) | B1 |
> | (c) | indication of an area calculated | C1 |
> |  | 6 × 9 = 54(m) | C1 |
> |  | ½ (6 × 9) = 27(m) | C1 |
> |  | <u>81(m)</u> | A1 |
> | (d) | horizontal line finishes at 10 seconds | B1 |
> |  | straight line to time zero in two seconds | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a) Many candidates answered this question well. Most recognised that the cyclist and runner were accelerating and the cyclist was faster.
>
> (b) This was answered correctly by almost all candidates. Those that gave an incorrect response usually suggested that the cyclist had stopped or was at rest.
>
> (c) This was not a well answered question. A very common incorrect response from weaker candidates was to multiply speed by time (12 × 9) in order to get 108 m. A number of candidates partially calculated correct responses giving final values of 54 m or 27 m.
>
> (d) There was a very high number of correct responses to this question. A common error was candidates drawing the correct line for the constant speed but then the deceleration line finishing at 4 m/s rather than 0 m/s.
