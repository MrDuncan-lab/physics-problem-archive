> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Прочитать термометр, определить используемое свойство жидкости и объяснить теплопроводность и конвекцию при нагревании воды.

A Bunsen burner heats a beaker of water, as shown in Fig. 4.1.

![[0625-s18-32-p5-f1.png]]

**Fig. 4.1**

**(a)** **(i)** Fig. 4.2 shows the thermometer used in Fig. 4.1.

State the temperature shown on the thermometer.

![[0625-s18-32-p5-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 4.2**

temperature =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ ° C **[1]**

**(ii)** The thermometer shown in Fig. 4.2 uses a physical property that changes with temperature.

Indicate the measurable property that changes with temperature. Tick **one** box.

![[0625-s18-32-p5-f3.svg]]

**[1]**

**(b)** Thermal energy (heat) transfers through the bottom of the beaker to the water.

State the name given to this process. **[1]**

**(c)** Thermal energy transfers throughout the water in the beaker.

Describe and explain how this happens. **[4]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | 38 (°C) | 1 |
> | (a)(ii) | 2nd box ticked i.e. expansion of liquid | 1 |
> | (b) | conduction | 1 |
> | (c) | any **three** from:<br>water is a fluid<br>water molecules gain (kinetic) energy/move faster/further apart<br>water expands/volume increases<br>warm/hot water or molecules rises<br>convection (current created)<br>cooler/cold/water falls/sinks (to be heated again) | 3 |
> |  | correct reference to density change of water | 1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a) (i)** Almost all candidates demonstrated that they could read the thermometer correctly at 38(°C).
>
> **(ii)** Most candidates identified expansion of liquid as the correct response.
>
> **(b)** Many candidates did not read the question carefully enough and gave convection as the answer. The question was asking about heat transfer through the beaker to the water not through the water.
>
> **(c)** A few candidates gave very clear step by step accounts of how convection takes place. However, many candidates gave very vague responses and some described conduction.
