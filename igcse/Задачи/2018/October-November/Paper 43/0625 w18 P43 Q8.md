# Question 8 (10 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2018 · Paper 4 — Theory (Extended), вариант 3 · вопрос 8  
Источник: [[_pdf/2018/0625_w18_qp_43.pdf#page=11|условие, стр. 11]] · [[_pdf/2018/0625_w18_ms_43.pdf#page=11|схема оценивания, стр. 11]] · [[_pdf/2018/0625_w18_er.pdf#page=34|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

Темы: [[1.7.1 Energy]], [[4.3.2 Series and parallel circuits]], [[4.2.4 Resistance]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Описать превращения энергии цепи, рассчитать ток, напряжение и сопротивление проволоки, учесть изменение её длины и диаметра.

A 9.0 V battery is connected to a 120 Ω resistor in series with wire P. Fig. 8.1 shows a voltmeter connected across the 120 Ω resistor.

![[0625-w18-43-p11-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 8.1**

**(a)** State the energy changes that are taking place in the circuit. **[2]**

**(b)** The reading on the voltmeter is 2.4 V.

Calculate:

**(i)** the current in the 120 Ω resistor

current =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(ii)** the potential difference (p.d.) across wire P

p.d. =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(iii)** the resistance of wire P.

resistance =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(c)** Wire P has a diameter *d* and a length *l*. A second piece of wire Q is made of the same material as P. The diameter of wire Q is 0.50 × *d* and its length is 5.0 × *l*.

Calculate the resistance of wire Q.

resistance =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[4]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(a)** from chemical (energy) to thermal / heat (energy) **and** as a result of electrical working
>
> **(b)(i)** 0.020 A
>
> **(c)** 6600 Ω

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Some candidates omitted any comment concerning the chemical (potential) energy that is stored in the battery.
>
> **(b) (i)** This was often correct and was awarded full credit.
>
> **(ii)** This was often correct but answers of 9.0 V and 2.4 V were given by some candidates.
>
> **(iii)** This calculation was usually correct.
>
> **(c)** Only stronger candidates answered fully correctly. Many candidates realised that increasing the length of the wire, increased its resistance and multiplied the answer to **(b)(iii)** by 5. The effect of halving the diameter of the wire was less well understood. A few candidates realised that the effect depended on the square of the diameter and divided by 4. However, more candidates realised that the effect was an inverse one and multiplied by a further 2. Very few candidates used the inverse proportion of the square of the diameter and full credit was only very occasionally awarded.
