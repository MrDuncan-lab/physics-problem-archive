> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Объяснить взаимодействие магнитов, описать намагничивание железной иглы и определение её полюсов.

Fig. 10.1 shows a demonstration with magnets.

![[0625-s17-31-p13-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 10. 1**

The strong magnet has a pole on its top surface and a pole on its bottom surface. The strong magnet is placed above a small magnet that is connected to a bench by a string.

**(a)** **(i)** Explain why the small magnet is in the position shown in Fig. 10.1. **[2]**

**(ii)** The strong magnet is turned so that the opposite surface is now facing the small magnet. State and explain what happens, if anything. **[1]**

**(b)** **(i)** Describe a method for magnetising an iron pin using a permanent magnet. **[2]**

**(ii)** Explain how you would identify the poles of the magnet made in **(b)(i)**. **[2]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | attraction/strong magnet pulling small magnet | B1 |
> |  | the two magnets have opposite poles facing each other | B1 |
> | (a)(ii) | drops/falls due **to** repulsion owtte | B1 |
> | (b)(i) | magnet stroked along pin/stroked in same direction by magnet/pin stroked using same pole of magnet | B1 |
> |  | procedure repeated/several times | B1 |
> | (b)(ii) | use a known magnet | B1 |
> |  | opposite poles attract **OR** like poles repel | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Middle and higher scoring candidates gained at least partial credit for recognising the attraction between the two magnets in **part (i)**. Very few gained full credit for **part (ii)**. A common misconception was that the magnets would attract.
>
> **(b) (i)** Many partially correct responses. A significant number of candidates confused magnetism with electrostatics gaining no credit.
>
> **(b) (ii)** Many responses that gained partial credit.
