# Question 3 (7 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2017 · Paper 3 — Theory (Core), вариант 2 · вопрос 3  
Источник: [[_pdf/2017/0625_s17_qp_32.pdf#page=4|условие, стр. 4–5]] · [[_pdf/2017/0625_s17_ms_32.pdf#page=3|схема оценивания, стр. 3]] · [[_pdf/2017/0625_s17_er.pdf#page=16|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Рассчитать вес навеса и момент его силы, указать способ уменьшения момента.

Fig. 3.1 shows a large sunshade.

![[0625-s17-32-p4-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 3.1**

The arm holding the sunshade pivots about the end of a support.

**(a)** The sunshade has a mass of 20.0 kg.

Calculate the weight of the sunshade.

weight =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ N **[3]**

**(b)** **(i)** Another sunshade is shown in Fig. 3.2. This sunshade weighs 180 N. The arm holding the sunshade extends 2.5 m from the pivot.

![[0625-s17-32-p4-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 3.2**

Calculate the moment of the sunshade about the pivot.

moment =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ N m **[3]**

**(ii)** How can the moment produced by the sunshade be reduced? Tick **one** box.

![[0625-s17-32-p5-f1.svg]]

by decreasing the height of the support

by decreasing the length of the arm holding the sunshade

by increasing the weight of the base

by increasing the weight of the sunshade **[1]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(a)** 200 (N)
>
> **(b)(i)** 450 (N m)

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | weight = mass × gravitational field strength in any form | C1 |
> |  | 20.0 × 10.0 | C1 |
> |  | 200 (N) | A1 |
> | (b)(i) | moment = force × (perpendicular) distance (from pivot) in any form | C1 |
> |  | 180.0 × 2.5 | C1 |
> |  | 450 (N m) | A1 |
> | (b)(ii) | 2nd box down ticked decrease the length of the arm holding the sun-shade | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** The vast majority of candidates scored this mark. A number of candidates still used g = 9.81 N / kg. A common error was to state weight = mass ÷ g.
>
> **(b) (i)** Most candidates correctly calculated the moment of the sunshade about the pivot. A common error was to divide the force by the distance, or to convert the force to kilograms.
>
> **(ii)** The vast majority of candidates scored this mark.
