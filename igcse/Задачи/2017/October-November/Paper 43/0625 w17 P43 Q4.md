# Question 4 (6 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2017 · Paper 4 — Theory (Extended), вариант 3 · вопрос 4  
Источник: [[_pdf/2017/0625_w17_qp_43.pdf#page=6|условие, стр. 6]] · [[_pdf/2017/0625_w17_ms_43.pdf#page=3|схема оценивания, стр. 3]] · [[_pdf/2017/0625_w17_er.pdf#page=35|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 w17 P43]]  
Темы: [[2.1.2 Particle model]], [[2.2.1 Thermal expansion of solids, liquids and gases]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Сравнить строение твёрдого тела и жидкости и объяснить первоначальное падение и последующий подъём жидкости при нагревании колбы.

**(a)** The molecules of most liquids are, on average, slightly further apart than the molecules of a solid.

State **one** other difference between the molecular structures of a solid and a liquid. **[1]**

**(b)** A glass tube passes through a stopper and into a glass flask. Fig. 4.1 shows that the flask is completely full of a liquid and that there is also some liquid in the tube.

![[0625-w17-43-p6-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 4.1**

The flask is immersed in a large beaker of very hot water. At first, the level of the liquid in the tube falls, but after a short time it rises.

**(i)** Explain why, at first, the level of the liquid in the tube falls. **[3]**

**(ii)** Explain why the liquid level in the tube stops falling and starts to rise. **[2]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | molecules of solid arranged in lattice / in organised pattern / without gaps / orderly / fixed structure | B1 |
> | (b)(i) | glass heated first **or** at first liquid not heated / does not expand / takes time (to heat up) **or** glass poor conductor | B1 |
> |  | glass expands | B1 |
> |  | capacity / volume of flask increases | B1 |
> | (b)(ii) | liquid (starts to) warms up | B1 |
> |  | liquid expands more than the solid / glass | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Most candidates were able to state one other difference between the molecular structure of a solid and that of a liquid. Answers that only referred to the difference already stated or that discussed the macroscopic behaviour of solids and liquids or that were not related to a structural difference could not be credited.
>
> **(b) (i)** Only the strongest candidates realised that the initial decrease in the liquid level was due to the expansion of the glass.
>
> **(ii)** Generally, this question was slightly better answered than **(i)** but many of the candidates who had already suggested that the increase in temperature of the liquid led to its contraction felt the need to be consistent and gave answers in terms of a decreasing temperature.
