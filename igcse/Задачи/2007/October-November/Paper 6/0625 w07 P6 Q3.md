He then moves the ammeter to new positions in the circuit and measures the current in each lamp in turn. The positions of the pointer on the ammeter scale are shown below.

Write down the ammeter readings *I*, *I*$_{1}$, *I*$_{2}$ and *I*$_{3}$. **[3]**

**(b)** Theory suggests that *I* = *I*$_{1}$ + *I*$_{2}$ + *I*$_{3}$. State whether or not your readings support this theory. Give a reason for your answer.

His reading is *V* = 1.6 V.

**(i)** Calculate the resistance *R* of the lamps arranged in parallel, using the equation

where *I* is the value of the current in **(a)(i)**.

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Many candidates were able correctly to read the meters and record the results. Some neglected to include the unit (A).
>
> **(b)** Candidates who were used to reviewing the results of experiments realised that the readings that they obtained in **part (a)** agreed with the theory within the limits of experimental error and were able to express that.
>
> **(c) and (d)** Many candidates correctly suggested a variable resistor and were able to calculate the resistance
>
> although some missed the unit (Ω). Most knew the symbol for a voltmeter but only those confident with its use positioned the voltmeter correctly on the circuit diagram.
