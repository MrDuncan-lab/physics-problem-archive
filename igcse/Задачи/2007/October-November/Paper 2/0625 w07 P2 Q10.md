> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Магнитное поле соленоида и намагничивание железных стержней.

A coil of insulated wire is connected in series with a battery, a resistor and a switch.

![[0625-w07-2-p14-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 10.1**

**(a)** The switch is closed and the current in the coil creates a magnetic field.

**(i)** On Fig. 10.1, draw the shape of the magnetic field, both inside and outside the coil. **[4]**

**(ii)** A glass bar, an iron bar and a perspex bar are placed in turn inside the coil.

Which one makes the field stronger?  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(b)** Two thin iron rods are placed inside the coil as shown in Fig. 10.2. The switch is then closed.

![[0625-w07-2-p14-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 10.2**

The iron rods move apart. Suggest why this happens. **[3]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(a)(i)** lines mostly parallel within coil
>
> **(a)(i)** pattern roughly symmetrical
>
> **(a)(i)** no lines touching or crossing
>
> **(b)** same direction

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Very few knew what the magnetic field of a coil looks like. Most attempts were poorly drawn, and large numbers simply did not answer this part. However it was rare to find a candidate who did not know that the iron bar would make the field stronger.
>
> **(b)** Most were able to score the mark for “rods repel each other”, but few scored much else. There was a huge proportion that talked about the rods being charged, and even went as far as making them positive or negative. This confusion between electrostatic charge and magnetism is quite common, a fact which teachers might care to note.
