> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Зависимость напряжения на термисторе от температуры при постоянном токе.

A thermistor connected to a variable voltage supply is immersed in a beaker of water.

The beaker of water is heated slowly, using a Bunsen burner, as shown in Fig. 9.1.

![[0625-w07-2-p12-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 9.1**

At different temperatures, the voltage is adjusted until the current is 25 mA and the value of the voltage is noted.

The results are shown below.

| temperature / °C | 15 | 30 | 45 | 60 | 75 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| voltage / V | 18.8 | 8.8 | 4.7 | 2.6 | 1.5 | 1.2 |

| temperature / °C | 15 | 30 | 45 | 60 | 75 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| voltage / V | 18.8 | 8.8 | 4.7 | 2.6 | 1.5 | 1.2 |

**(a)** On the axes of Fig. 9.2, shown on page 13, plot a graph of voltage against temperature. **[4]**

![[0625-w07-2-p13-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 9.2**

**(b)** From the graph, find the voltage needed to give a current of 25 mA

**(i)** at 40 °C, voltage needed =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ V **[1]**

**(ii)** at 80 °C. voltage needed =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ V **[1]**

**(c)** Use your results in **(b)** to calculate the resistance of the thermistor

**(i)** at 40 °C,

resistance at 40 °C =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(ii)** at 80 °C.

resistance at 80 °C =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[5]**

**(d)** Use your results in **(c)** to complete the following sentence about thermistors of the sort used in this experiment.

The thermistor in this experiment is a device whose resistance  \_\_\_\_\_\_

as the temperature increases. **[1]**

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Points were usually well plotted, but the curves through the points were often very careless. The last comment made for **7(a)** would apply here also.
>
> **(b)** Values were usually within the required limits.
>
> (5.3 – 6.1, 0.9 – 1.7)
>
> **(c)** A good number knew how to calculate the resistance from the voltage and current, but the vast majority did not take into account that the current was given in mA and not A. Hence answers were usually wrong by a factor of10$^{3}$. There were the usual responses who thought that R =I/V or V x I.
>
> (220 – 240, 40 – 60)
>
> **(d)** All that was required here was an appropriate deduction from **part (c)**. Candidates usually answered this correctly.
