# Question 11 (4 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2007 · Paper 2 — Core theory · вопрос 11  
Источник: [[_pdf/2007/0625_w07_qp_2.pdf#page=15|условие, стр. 15]] · [[_pdf/2007/0625_w07_ms_2.pdf#page=6|схема оценивания, стр. 6]] · [[_pdf/2007/0625_w07_er.pdf#page=3|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Период полураспада по таблице активности и сравнение образцов.

The activity of a sample of radioactive material is determined every 10 minutes for an hour. The results are shown in the table.

| time / minutes | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| activity count / s | 461 | 332 | 229 | 162 | 106 | 81 | 51 |

| time / minutes | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| activity count / s | 461 | 332 | 229 | 162 | 106 | 81 | 51 |

**(a)** From the figures in the table, estimate the half-life of the radioactive material.

half-life =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ minutes **[1]**

**(b)** A second experiment is carried out with another sample of the same material. At the start of the experiment, this sample has twice the number of atoms as the first sample.

Suggest what values might be obtained for

**(i)** the activity at the start of the second experiment,

\_\_\_\_\_\_ count / s **[1]**

**(ii)** the half-life of the material in the second experiment.

\_\_\_\_\_\_ minutes **[1]**

**(c)** Name one type of particle that the material might be emitting in order to cause this activity. **[1]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | within range 18–20 (mins) | B1 |
> | (b)(i) | 922 or thereabouts | B1 |
> | (b)(ii) | his (a) | B1 |
> | (c) | alpha OR beta | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Half lives were usually correctly estimated.
>
> (18 – 20 mins)
>
> **(b)** The usual misunderstandings about half life were evident in answers to this, but it was pleasing that a good proportion did get at least **(i)** correct. Answers to **(ii)** were often half or double the appropriate value.
>
> **(c)** Either alpha or beta would have scored the mark here, and there were good numbers who did, but there were far too many answers like “gamma particles”.
