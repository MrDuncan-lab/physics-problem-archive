> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** The vast majority of candidates gained credit, although there were some incorrect values given, of which ’14 °C’’ and ‘21°C’ were the most common incorrect values.
>
> **(b)** The units were done well and it only a few candidates got the temperature symbol wrong. Omission of one or both units was the biggest cause for loss of credit, but this was by only a small minority of candidates.
>
> **(c)** Most candidates gained credit here, with only a few omitting or giving an incorrect unit (even on low scoring scripts). There were only a few incorrect values, such as 12.5 or 14 cm$^{3}$.
>
> **(d)** A majority of candidates gained some credit for the estimate, with many also gaining credit for a good accompanying explanation.
>
> 47 © 2013
>
> Quite a number used 78°C as their starting point to give an answer outside the allowed range but still gained some credit. Some candidates were quite confused and gave the difference between 74 and 66 as the answer.
>
> **(e)** Many standard answers were seen, variations of ‘the same room temperature’ being most common.
>
> Common errors involved leaving out the word ‘initial’ or not saying which of the water samples should have the same volume. There were many references to equipment or a second environmental condition and there were also candidates giving extra incorrect answers which negated their only correct answer.
