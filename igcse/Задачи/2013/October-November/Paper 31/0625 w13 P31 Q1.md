# Question 1 (7 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2013 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 1 · вопрос 1  
Источник: [[_pdf/2013/0625_w13_qp_31.pdf#page=2|условие, стр. 2–3]] · [[_pdf/2013/0625_w13_ms_31.pdf#page=4|схема оценивания, стр. 4]] · [[_pdf/2013/0625_w13_er.pdf#page=18|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 w13 P31]]  
Темы: [[1.5.1 Effects of forces]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Закон Гука жёсткость пружины и предел пропорциональности.

**(a)** State Hooke’s law. **[1]**

**(b)** Fig. 1.1 shows a graph of the stretching force *F* acting on a spring against the extension *x* of the spring.

![[0625-w13-31-p2-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 1.1**

**(i)** State the features of the graph that show that the spring obeys Hooke’s law. **[1]**

**(ii)** Calculate *k*, the force per unit extension of the spring.

*k* =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[3]**

**(iii)** The limit of proportionality of the spring is reached at an extension of 50 mm.

Continue the graph in Fig. 1.1 to suggest how the spring behaves when the stretching force is increased to values above 125 N. **[1]**

**(iv)** Another spring has a smaller value of *k*. This spring obeys Hooke’s law for extensions up to 80 mm.

On the grid of Fig. 1.1, draw a possible line of the variation of *F* with *x* for this spring. **[1]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)(ii)** 2.5 N / mm OR 2500 N / m

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | extension (of spring) proportional to load / force (applied)<br>OR load / force (applied) proportional to extension<br>OR force = constant × extension<br>OR extension = constant × force<br>OR F = kx in any form with symbols explained | B1 |
> | (b)(i) | graph is through the origin AND is a straight line / has a constant gradient | B1 |
> | (b)(ii) | F = kx in any form OR (k =) F/x | C1 |
> |  | use of a point anywhere on graph e.g. 50 / 20 | C1 |
> |  | 2.5 N / mm OR 2500 N / m | A1 |
> | (b)(iii) | from 50 mm extension, graph curves with no negative gradient | B1 |
> | (b)(iv) | straight line through origin with smaller gradient than graph shown finishing<br>at more than 50 mm | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Most candidates could state an acceptable version of Hooke’s law. Those who expressed the law as a formula needed to explain the symbols used.
>
> **(b) (i)** A graph representing a direct proportion is a straight line, or a line with a constant gradient, and it passes through the origin. In many answers one or other of these requirements was omitted.
>
> **(ii)** It was unusual for candidates not to be able to calculate the correct numerical value of *k*. However, a large proportion lost some credit by omitting the unit.
>
> **(iii)** The continuation of the graph needed to be a curve with a positive gradient. Most candidates made a good attempt.
>
> **(iv)** Most answers correctly showed a straight line through the origin. A good proportion of the candidates also correctly drew the line with a smaller gradient than the original one.
