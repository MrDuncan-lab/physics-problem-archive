**(a)** An atom consists of a nucleus made up of protons and neutrons, surrounded by orbiting electrons.

**(i)** Which of these particles has a positive charge?  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(ii)** Which two of these particles have almost equal mass?

\_\_\_\_\_\_ and  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(b)** A silver nucleus is denoted by$^{107}_{47}$ Ag. State the number of protons and the number of neutrons in this nucleus.

number of protons =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ number of neutrons =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(c)** The graph in Fig. 11.1 shows part of the decay curve of a radioactive nuclide. The count rate is plotted against time.

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)**
>
> **(i)** Most candidates could identify the proton as having a positive charge.
>
> **(ii)** All the possible pairings of the three particles were quoted, with rather more choosing the correct than either of the other possibilities.
>
> **(b)** A very large majority of candidates knew the meanings of the numbers preceding the symbol of the element and could do the arithmetic.
>
> **(c)**
>
> **(i)** Precise knowledge of the meaning of half-life and careful use of the graph was required. The lack of one or other of these requirements meant that many errors were made. Those with no real idea wrote down a count rate rather than a time.
>
> **(ii)** It was possible to gain full credit for any half-life value quoted in **(i)**, with the error carried forward. However, it tended to be those with the correct value or one very close to 8 hours who were successful.
