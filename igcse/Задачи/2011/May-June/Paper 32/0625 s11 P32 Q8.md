# Question 8 (8 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2011 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 2 · вопрос 8

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> ЭДС, ток и сопротивление ламп при разных соединениях.

**(a)** What is meant by the *electromotive force* (e.m.f.) of an electric power supply? **[2]**

**(b)** When connected to a 240 V supply, a desk lamp has a power rating of 60 W.

Calculate

**(i)** the current in the lamp,

current =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(ii)** the resistance of the lamp’s filament.

resistance =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(c)** A torch lamp is normally connected to a 3.0 V battery and carries a current of 0.25 A. The resistance of its filament is 12 Ω.

The desk lamp in **(b)** and the torch lamp are connected in series.

Students X and Y plan to connect the lamp combination to a 240 V supply.

Student X says that the filament of the torch lamp will melt and the circuit will no longer work. Student Y says that both lamps will light up and stay on.

Show, with a suitable calculation, whether student X or student Y is correct. **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)(i)** = 0.25 A OR ¼ A
>
> **(b)(ii)** OR (R = )240/0.25 OR P=V$^{2}$/R or other version e.g. (R=) V$^{2}$/P OR (R=) 240$^{2}$/60 R= 960 Ω

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Candidates have no doubt been taught that the term electromotive force is misleading, but many of them continue to describe the quantity as the force which drives a current. An acceptable meaning at this level is given in the syllabus: i.e. the energy supplied by a source (1 marking point) in driving charge round a complete circuit (1 marking point). The acceptable alternative was ‘the voltage or p.d. across a source (1 marking point) when not connected to a circuit (1 marking point). Some variation in the wording was allowed in either case.
>
> **(b)** In **(i)** and **(ii)** the answers were almost invariably correct.
>
> **(c)** This was a testing question making demands on the thought processes of even the most able candidates. Most of those who did tackle the problem calculated the current in the circuit with the lamps in series, arrived at a value for the current just below the normal operating current of both bulbs, and drew the correct conclusion. The alternative approach, used by a few who knew the appropriate formula, was to calculate the p.d. across each lamp, showing that these were just below the normal operating p.d. of both bulbs.
