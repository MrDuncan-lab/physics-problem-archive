> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Взаимодействие заряженных шаров и электризация пластмассы трением.

**(a)** Two light, identical spheres, A and B, are suspended alongside each other on thin nylon threads, as shown in Fig. 7.1.

![[0625-s11-21-p11-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 7.1**

A is given a positive charge and B is given a negative charge.

On Fig. 7.1, draw how the threads and spheres might look after the spheres have been charged. **[1]**

**(b)** A cleaner is attempting to remove dust from some plastic-covered furniture, using a dry cloth. Unfortunately, this seems to make the dust cling more firmly to the plastic covering.

**(i)** Suggest why this happens. **[3]**

**(ii)** Suggest why this would be less likely to happen if the cleaner used a cloth which was very slightly damp. **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)(i)** <u>plastic/furniture attracts dust/fluff</u>

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | spheres closer together allow touching spheres | B1 |
> | (b)(i) | charging (of anything) by friction/rubbing | B1 |
> |  | plastic/furniture (becomes) charged OR electron/charge transfer | M1 |
> |  | <u>plastic/furniture attracts dust/fluff</u> | A1 |
> | (b)(ii) | idea of charge leaking | B1 |
> |  | water is a conductor | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Most had sufficient understanding of electrostatics to know that the two spheres would move towards each other. Many of the threads were poorly drawn, but this was not penalised on this occasion.
>
> **(b) (c)** The ability to apply the theory of electrostatics to a real situation was in much shorter supply, but nevertheless some candidates made brave attempts. Most were not sure what was being charged by the action of rubbing, and some seemed to think that like charges attract each other. Credit was given for anything relevant that was written, and many candidates scored partial credit.
