# Question 7 (6 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2011 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 2 · вопрос 7  
Источник: [[_pdf/2011/0625_w11_qp_32.pdf#page=10|условие, стр. 10]] · [[_pdf/2011/0625_w11_ms_32.pdf#page=5|схема оценивания, стр. 5]] · [[_pdf/2011/0625_w11_er.pdf#page=20|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 w11 P32]]  
Темы: [[3.4 Sound]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Скорость звука, частота волны и расстояние до грозы.

**(a)** State an approximate value for

**(i)** the speed of sound in air,  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(ii)** the speed of light in air \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(b)** Use your value from **(a)(i)** to calculate the frequency of a sound wave that has a wavelength of 1.2 m.

frequency =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(c)** A meteorologist observes an approaching thunderstorm and records a time difference of 4.8 s between seeing a lightning flash and hearing the thunder that follows.

**(i)** Calculate the distance of the thunderstorm from the meteorologist.

distance =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(ii)** State an assumption you made when calculating this distance. **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)** correct evaluation of candidate’s (a)(i)/1.2 (330 m/s gives 275 Hz)

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | 320-350 m/s condone 100 – 999 m/s | B1 |
> | (a)(ii) | 3 × 10$^{8}$ m/s condone 2 – 4 × 10$^{8}$ m/s | B1 |
> | (b) | <u>use of</u> v = fλ | C1 |
> |  | correct evaluation of candidate’s (a)(i)/1.2<br>(330 m/s gives 275 Hz) | A1 |
> | (c)(i) | correct evaluation of candidate’s (a)(i) × 4.8<br>(330 m/s gives 1584m) | B1 |
> | (c)(ii) | <u>clear statement that light travels instantaneously o.w.t.t.e.</u><br>OR distance of thunderstorm same as distance travelled by sound<br>OR thunder and lightning caused by same event<br>OR negligible wind | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a) The speeds of sound and light were usually correctly quoted, with the most common errors being incorrect units or the incorrect power of 10.
>
> (b) Most candidates carried out a correct calculation with errors in the rearrangement of the wave equation, or the use of the speed of the wrong type of wave, accounting for the vast majority of mistakes.
>
> (c) Again, most calculations were carried out correctly. More able candidates gained credit for clearly stating that their calculation assumed that light had arrived instantaneously. Many candidates simply stated that light travels faster than sound or made statements about the situation that were too vague to gain credit.
