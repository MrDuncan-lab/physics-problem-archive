# Question 4 (6 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2011 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 2 · вопрос 4  
Источник: [[_pdf/2011/0625_w11_qp_32.pdf#page=7|условие, стр. 7]] · [[_pdf/2011/0625_w11_ms_32.pdf#page=5|схема оценивания, стр. 5]] · [[_pdf/2011/0625_w11_er.pdf#page=20|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 w11 P32]]  
Темы: [[2.1.3 Gases and the absolute scale of temperature]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Давление гелия при охлаждении и расширении воздушного шара.

A sealed balloon containing some helium gas is released and rises into the upper atmosphere. As the balloon rises the temperature of the helium falls and the balloon expands.

Explain, in terms of atoms,

**(a)** the effect of the fall in temperature on the helium pressure, **[3]**

**(b)** the effect of the expansion of the balloon on the helium pressure. **[3]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | molecules/atoms move more slowly | B1 |
> |  | <u>fewer collisions OR less hard collisions with walls / balloon</u> | B1 |
> |  | lower pressure | B1 |
> | (b) | larger surface area of walls OR atoms further apart OR atoms travel further | B1 |
> |  | <u>fewer collisions with walls/balloon (only penalise missing walls once in</u> (a) or (b)) | B1 |
> |  | lower pressure | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> Most candidates gained partial credit in each of the two parts, with many well-reasoned answers. Some candidates did not answer in terms of atoms or molecules moving or spoke about collisions without being more specific that the collisions were with the walls. Weaker candidates were more interested in external pressures and bursting balloons rather than concentrating on the helium pressure, and a surprising number deduced that the helium pressure would rise in one or both cases.
>
> (a) Candidates who appreciated that the KE would be less needed to progress this by saying that the speed of the atoms would decrease. Many candidates appreciated that a lower frequency of collisions would lead to a fall in pressure.
>
> (b) More space or increasing volume were popular answers but were not sufficient to gain credit as this was in effect merely repeating the question. Many candidates appreciated that fewer collisions with the walls was the key point.
