# Question 6 (7 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2011 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 1 · вопрос 6

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Длина звуковой волны и скорость звука в стали.

**(a)** Fig. 6.1 shows the position of layers of air, at one moment, as a sound wave of constant frequency passes through the air. Compressions are labelled C. Rarefactions are labelled R.

![[0625-w11-31-p7-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 6.1**

**(i)** State how Fig. 6.1 would change if

**1**. the sound had a higher frequency, **[1]**

**2**. the sound were louder. **[2]**

**(ii)** On Fig. 6.1, draw a line marked with arrows at each end to show the wavelength of the sound. **[1]**

**(b)** In an experiment to measure the speed of sound in steel, a steel pipe of length 200 m is struck at one end with a hammer. A microphone at the other end of the pipe is connected to an accurate timer. The timer records a delay of 0.544 s between the arrival of the sound transmitted by the steel pipe and the sound transmitted by the air in the pipe.

The speed of sound in air is 343 m / s. Calculate the speed of sound in steel.

speed of sound in steel =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[3]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)** 5128 m/s

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | 1. compressions and/or rarefactions closer together<br>OR more compressions and/or rarefactions | B1 |
> |  | ignore wavelength shorter<br>2. layers closer together at compressions | B1 |
> |  | layers farther apart at rarefactions | B1 |
> |  | OR<br>compressions narrower | (B1) |
> |  | rarefactions wider<br>ignore wavelength shorter ignore ‘amplitude greater’ ignore ‘maximum<br>displacement greater’ | (B1) |
> | (a)(ii) | distance between 2 compressions or 2 rarefactions shown with reasonable<br>accuracy | B1 |
> | (b) | time taken by sound in air = 200 / 343 = 0.583 s | C1 |
> |  | time taken by sound in steel = 0.583 – 0.544 = 0.039 s | C1 |
> |  | 5128 m/s | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a)
>
> (i) 1. A good proportion of candidates could suggest that compressions (or rarefactions) would be
>
> closer together or that there would be more of them.
>
> 2. This part question proved to be difficult for most candidates, largely because many seemed
>
> unfamiliar with the representation of a longitudinal wave shown in the figure. Very few correctly referred to layers of air being closer together at compressions and further apart at rarefactions.
>
> (ii) Candidates who chose to mark the distance between adjacent compressions or rarefactions usually gained credit. Those who chose to show the wavelength other than in this way were less successful, sometimes through carelessness.
>
> (b) Most candidates found it difficult to take the appropriate steps to calculate the speed in steel, with some showing no coherent steps. Some successfully gained credit by calculating the time taken by the sound travelling in air. Many of these then added, rather than subtracted, the time delay. The final answer achieved depended on how many significant figures were used in the calculation; good practice would be not to apply rounding until the final answer stage of a multi-step calculation, but no penalties were applied.
