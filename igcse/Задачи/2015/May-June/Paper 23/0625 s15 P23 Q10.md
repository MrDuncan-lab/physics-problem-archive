# Question 10 (6 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2015 · Paper 2 — Core theory, вариант 3 · вопрос 10  
Источник: [[_pdf/2015/0625_s15_qp_23.pdf#page=14|условие, стр. 14–15]] · [[_pdf/2015/0625_s15_ms_23.pdf|схема оценивания]] · [[_pdf/2015/0625_s15_er.pdf#page=16|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 s15 P23]]  
Темы: [[4.2.1 Electric charge]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Электризация трением и взаимодействие пластиковых полосок.

A student is investigating static electricity.

**(i)** The student charges a plastic rod by rubbing it with a dry cloth. The rod becomes positively charged as shown in Fig. 10.1.

![[0625-s15-23-p14-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 10.1**

**1.** State the sign of the charge, if any, on the cloth. **[1]**

**2.** State which particles the cloth has gained for it to have this charge. **[1]**

**(ii)** The student then rubs two thin plastic strips with another dry cloth. The strips are suspended so that they are free to move.

Fig. 10.2 shows the two plastic strips before and after the student rubs them with the dry cloth.

![[0625-s15-23-p14-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 10.2**

Explain why the bottom ends of the strips move apart after rubbing. **[2]**

**(iii)** A boy wears a shirt made from nylon. After wearing the shirt for some time he finds the shirt clinging to his body.

Use your knowledge of electrostatic charges to explain why this occurs. **[2]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (i) | 1. negative | B1 |
> |  | 2. electrons | B1 |
> | (ii) | (both) strips have same (type of) charge | B1 |
> |  | (and so) repel (each other) | B1 |
> | (iii) | (idea of) shirt gaining / losing (electric) charge OR becomes charged<br>OR charge transferred between shirt and body | B1 |
> |  | unlike charges attract | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> Many candidates found this question on electrostatics to be challenging.
>
> (i) Most candidates were able to state that the cloth would be negatively charged; better candidates were able to name the electron as the particles that had been transferred to the cloth.
>
> (ii) The more able candidates scored full credit; for others, explanations were lacking in detail or incorrectly referred to magnetic poles.
>
> (iii) Few candidates scored full credit; many candidates did not go beyond the idea that the shirt became charged.
