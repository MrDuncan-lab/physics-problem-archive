**(b)** A student pours 100 cm$^{3}$ of hot water into a beaker. He records the temperature θ$_{\text{H}}$ of the hot water in the beaker.

θ$_{\text{H}}$ = 83 °C

State one precaution that you would take to ensure that the temperature reading is as reliable as possible. **[1]**

**(c)** The student adds 100 cm$^{3}$ of cold water to the hot water in the beaker. He records the temperature θ$_{\text{M}}$of the mixture.

θ$_{\text{M}}$ = 46 °C

Calculate the average temperature θ$_{\text{A}}$ of the hot water and the cold water using the equation

θ$_{\text{A}}$= $\dfrac{( \theta _{H} + \theta _{C} )}{2}$ .

θ$_{\text{A}}$=  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(d)** The student carried out this experiment carefully.

He was expecting that the temperature θ$_{\text{M}}$ of the mixture would be the same as the average temperature θ$_{\text{A}}$ of the hot water and the cold water.

Suggest two factors that could cause θ$_{\text{M}}$ and θ$_{\text{A}}$to be different.

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | θ$_{\text{C}}$ = 22 °C | 1 |
> | (b) | view thermometer at right angles OR stirring OR wait for reading to stop rising OR<br>thermometer (bulb) not touching sides / bottom of beaker owtte | 1 |
> | (c) | θ$_{\text{A}}$ = 52.5 (°C) OR e.c.f. | 1 |
> | (d) | any two from:<br>• heat loss to surroundings / beaker OR heat loss / drop in temperature by evaporation<br>• delays in taking readings<br>• reference to uncertainty in volume measurements | 2 |
> | (e)(i) | 78 (cm$^{3}$) | 1 |
> | (e)(ii) | EITHER:<br>Student 1 (80) – read to top of meniscus OR scale not read at right angles<br>OR Student 2 (79) – divisions are every 2(cm$^{3}$) not 1(cm$^{3}$)<br>OR Student 2 (79) – scale not read at right angles | 1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a) Most candidates recorded the correct value (22 °C) for room temperature. Some recorded 20.2 °C.
>
> (b) The majority of candidates made a relevant suggestion.
>
> (c) Most candidates calculated the temperature change successfully.
>
> (d) Many candidates found this part challenging. Stronger candidates referred correctly to heat loss to the surroundings, delays in taking readings or uncertainty about the exact volume of cold water. Some answers hinted at the correct response but were too vague to be credited.
>
> (e) Most candidates could identify the correct reading. Many could explain the error made in taking the reading at the bottom of the meniscus. Fewer used the alternative correct response, explaining the scale divisions.
