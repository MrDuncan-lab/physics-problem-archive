# Question 1 (7 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2015 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 3 · вопрос 1  
Источник: [[_pdf/2015/0625_w15_qp_33.pdf#page=2|условие, стр. 2]] · [[_pdf/2015/0625_w15_ms_33.pdf#page=4|схема оценивания, стр. 4]] · [[_pdf/2015/0625_w15_er.pdf#page=27|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> График падения шарика с сопротивлением воздуха и равнодействующая сил.

A plastic ball is dropped from the balcony of a tall building and falls towards the ground in a straight line.

Fig. 1.1 is the speed-time graph for the falling ball.

![[0625-w15-33-p2-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 1.1**

**(a)** State and explain, in terms of forces, what is happening to the speed of the ball between time *t* = 2.0 s and *t* = 3.0 s. **[2]**

**(b)** On Fig. 1.1, mark a point P on the line where the acceleration of the ball is not constant. **[1]**

**(c)** Using Fig. 1.1,

**(i)** calculate the acceleration of the ball between *t* = 0 s and *t* = 0.25 s,

acceleration =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(ii)** estimate the distance that the ball falls in the first 3.0 s.

distance =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(c)(ii)** between 16.5 and 17.1 m (inclusive)

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | speed is constant / uniform / unchanging OR terminal velocity / speed | B1 |
> |  | no net / resultant force OR air resistance cancels / equals weight | B1 |
> | (b) | P between 0.25 s and 1.90 s (inclusive) | B1 |
> | (c)(i) | (a =) ∆v / t OR 2.5 / 0.25 OR other point on correct section of line | B1 |
> |  | 9.6 to 10 m / s$^{2}$ (inclusive) | B1 |
> | (c)(ii) | area under graph OR attempt at counting squares OR between 16.2 and 17.5 m<br>(inclusive) | C1 |
> |  | between 16.5 and 17.1 m (inclusive) | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a) This was commonly well answered, but there were some candidates who simply stated what happened to the speed and offered no explanation in terms of the forces. The forces acting are equal was not a sufficiently precise explanation.
>
> (b) The point P was almost universally correctly positioned, although it was not always labelled.
>
> (c) (i) Many candidates obtained a correct answer by a suitable method and obtained full credit. There were those who misread the scale of the graph; the point (0.25, 2.5) was occasionally taken as (0.25, 2.25).
>
> (ii) This part of the question was less well answered. Many candidates tried to apply the formula x = vt rather than making an estimate of the area under the graph.
