# Question 4 (8 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2015 · Paper 3 — Extended theory, вариант 3 · вопрос 4

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Превращения энергии падающего и отскакивающего резинового шарика.

A soft rubber ball of mass 0.15 kg is dropped, in a vacuum, from a height of 2.0 m on to a hard surface. The ball then bounces.

**(a)** State the main energy changes taking place when

**(i)** the ball is falling,

**(ii)** the ball hits the surface and is changing shape,

**(iii)** the ball is regaining its shape and is rising from the surface. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed with which the ball hits the surface.

speed =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[4]**

**(c)** After rebounding from the surface, the ball rises to a height of 1.9 m.

Suggest why the height to which the ball rises is less than the height from which the ball falls. **[1]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)** 6.3(24555) m / s

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | gravitational (potential energy) to kinetic (energy) | B1 |
> | (a)(ii) | kinetic (energy) to elastic / strain (potential energy) | B1 |
> | (a)(iii) | elastic / strain (potential energy) to kinetic (energy) | B1 |
> | (b) | mgh OR 0.15 × 10 × 2.0 OR 3(.0 J) | C1 |
> |  | $^{1}_{2}$ mv$^{2}$ OR v$^{2}$ = 2gh | C1 |
> |  | v$^{2}$ = 2 × 3.0 / 0.15 OR 40 | C1 |
> |  | 6.3(24555) m / s | A1 |
> | (c) | heat / thermal / internal energy lost OR ball / surface gains heat / thermal / internal energy | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a) Energy changes were asked for in this part and in each of the three cases an initial energy and a final energy was expected. A significant number of candidates suggested only one type of energy in some or all of the three parts.
>
> (b) This calculation resulted in a wide variety of answers. Many candidates knew the correct formula, made the correct substitution and gave the correctly calculated answer with the unit. It is worth noting that there were some candidates who wrote down the formula KE = ½ mv$^{2}$, but omitted the square from either the substitution or from the calculation. It is, of course, possible to obtain the correct answer using the equations of kinematics but this approach can be more complicated and only occasionally did such candidates obtain full credit when taking this approach.
>
> (c) Many candidates had some idea of what was expected, but not all of these gave sufficiently detailed or exact suggestions.
