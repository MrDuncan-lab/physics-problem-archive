# Question 1 (13 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2002 · Paper 3 — Extended theory · вопрос 1  
Источник: [[_pdf/2002/0625_w02_qp_3.pdf#page=2|условие, стр. 2–3]] · [[_pdf/2002/0625_w02_ms_3.pdf|схема оценивания]] · [[_pdf/2002/0625_w02_er.pdf#page=1|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> График движения бруска, работа, кинетическая энергия и различие скорости и вектора скорости.

Fig. 1.1 shows a smooth metal block about to slide down BD, along DE and up EF. BD and DE are friction-free surfaces, but EF is rough. The block stops at F.

![[0625-w02-3-p2-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 1.1**

**(a)** On Fig. 1.2, sketch the speed-time graph for the journey from B to F. Label D, E and F on your graph. **[3]**

![[0625-w02-3-p2-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 1.2**

**(b)** The mass of the block is 0.2 kg. The vertical height of B above A is 0.6 m. The acceleration due to gravity is 10 m/s$^{2}$.

**(i)** Calculate the work done in lifting the block from A to B.

work done =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(ii)** At C, the block is moving at a speed of 2.5 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy at C.

kinetic energy =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[5]**

**(c)** As it passes D, the speed of the block remains almost constant but the velocity changes. Using the terms *vector* and *scalar*, explain this statement. **[2]**

**(d)** F is the point where the kinetic energy of the block is zero. In terms of energy changes, explain why F is lower than B. **[3]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)(i)** work = (2 × 0.6) = 1.2 J*
>
> **(b)(ii)** = 0.625 J*

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |

> | (a) | BD correct, (straight line i.e. constant acceleration)<br>Accept D & E marked on time axis<br>No labels -1 | B1 |
> |  | DE correct, (constant speed or slightly reducing speed only) | B1 |
> |  | EF correct, (speed reduced to zero, gradient steeper than BD) | B1 |
> | (b)(i) | force = 2 (N) | C1 |
> |  | work = (2 × 0.6) = 1.2 J* | A1 |
> | (b)(ii) | k.e. = 0.5mv$^{2}$ | C1 |

> | (c) | velocity - vector, speed scalar | B1 |
> |  | direction changes so velocity changes | B1 |
> | (d) | max 3:<br>work done against friction | B1 |
> |  | (more) friction on EF | B1 |
> |  | (k)e. changed to heat | B1 |
> |  | less k.e. changed to p.e. | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a) Generally well answered, but a number spoilt their attempt by not adding any labels as the question directed. Very few showed a steeper gradient for EF than BD. Weaker candidates often drew a “hump” shape which was not creditworthy.
>
> (b)(i) Many correct solutions with the most common error using mass x distance instead of force x distance.
>
> (ii) Surprisingly this was answered better than (i) with the majority of candidates scoring full marks. Common errors were to fail to square the velocity either at the formula stage or at the substitution stage.
>
> (c) Most knew the difference between scalar and vector quantities but many failed to relate this knowledge to the question asked. Few clearly stated that the direction changed at D but the value did not and so the velocity (vector) changed.
>
> (d) Few marks were scored because candidates failed to answer the question set. Many understood that the potential energy at F was less than the potential energy at B, but failed to say what had become of the apparent energy loss. Some indication that work done against the friction force along EF was converted to heat was all that was needed but this was rarely seen. Too many candidates confined their answers to the change from potential energy to kinetic energy along BD which contributed little to the answer.
>
> Answers: (b)(i) 1.2 J, (ii) 0.63 J.
