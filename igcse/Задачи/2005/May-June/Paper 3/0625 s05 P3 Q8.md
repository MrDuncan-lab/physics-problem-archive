> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Опыт для измерения сопротивления и расчёт заряда и мощности.

A student has a power supply, a resistor, a voltmeter, an ammeter and a variable resistor.

**(a)** The student obtains five sets of readings from which he determines an average value for the resistance of the resistor.

In the space below, draw a labelled diagram of a circuit that he could use. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe how the circuit should be used to obtain the five sets of readings. **[2]**

**(c)** Fig. 8.1 shows another circuit.

![[0625-s05-3-p11-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 8.1**

When the circuit is switched on, the ammeter reads 0.50 A.

**(i)** Calculate the value of the unknown resistor.

resistance =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(ii)** Calculate the charge passing through the 3.0 Ω resistor in 120 s.

charge =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[1]**

**(iii)** Calculate the power dissipated in the 3.0 Ω resistor.

power =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(c)(i)** R = 9(.0) Ω
>
> **(c)(iii)** = 0.75 W

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | circuit which would work with supply and resistor | B1 |
> |  | voltmeter in parallel and ammeter in series with resistor | B1 |
> |  | variable resistor in series or means of changing p.d. across<br>resistor | B1 |
> | (b) | read ammeter and voltmeter | B1 |
> |  | adjust rheostat/supply | B1 |
> | (c)(i) | I = V/R or V = IR or R = V/I or 0.5 = 6.0/3.0 + R | C1 |
> |  | R = 9(.0) Ω | A1 |
> | (c)(ii) | 60 C | B1 |
> | (c)(iii) | P = VI or = I$^{2}$R or P = v$^{2}$/R or (0.5 x 3.0) x 0.5 | C1 |
> |  | = 0.75 W | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a) The quality of the circuit diagrams was poor and labels were either inadequate or missing. All the usual faults were seen in large numbers such as voltmeters in series and ammeters in parallel, whilst some had extra connections which rendered the circuit useless. Some circuits had no fixed resistance shown which made it impossible to give credit.
>
> (b) Clear correct answers were hard to find. Many thought that changing the setting of the variable resistor changed only the current in the resistor whilst the potential difference across the resistor remained constant.
>
> (c)(i) The majority quoted and used Ohm’s Law, but some did not calculate the required resistance.
>
> (ii) The majority correctly used charge = current x time. As expected a number gave the wrong unit.
>
> (iii) The majority correctly used the formula for power, but large numbers used the wrong current. Wrong or missing units were not uncommon.
>
> Answers: (c)(i) 9.0 Ω, (ii) 60 C, (iii) 0.75 W.
