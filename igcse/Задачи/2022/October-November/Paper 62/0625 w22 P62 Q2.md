> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** The normal to the middle of the mirror was almost always drawn accurately and in the correct place.
>
> **(b)** Most candidates drew a line 7.0 cm long to represent the incident ray in the correct quadrant of **Fig. 2.1**, and at an angle of incidence of α = 30° to the normal, as instructed. The most common error was to use the protractor incorrectly and draw a line 7.0 cm long at α = 60°. Despite the instruction to label the end of the line with the letter A, the label was often missing.
>
> **(c)** For accurate ray tracing, the positions of pins P1 and P2 should be at least 5.0 cm apart. Most candidates suggested a distance greater than this and within the accepted tolerance range of between 5.0 cm and 15.0 cm.
>
> **(d)** Angle β was usually measured correctly to ± 2°, the tolerance allowed for the measurement of the angle. The units of angles were usually included in the table headings. However, some candidates gave °C as the unit.
>
> **(e)** The values of (α + β) for both angles of incidence α, were almost always calculated correctly.
>
> **(f)** Only stronger candidates were able to suggest a relationship between the two values of (α + β) they had just calculated. These candidates identified that the two values of (α + β) were the same (allowing for experimental error). Answers such as that the values of (α + β) are close / almost equal / not too far apart, were also accepted.
>
> **(g)** There were few completely correct answers to this part. Candidates were asked to suggest further values for the angle of incidence α that they could use to extend the investigation. Many correctly suggested values of α \> 45° (and \< 90°), and gained credit. Far fewer suggested any angle α \< 30°. A sizeable minority of candidates quoted values of α \> 90°, which showed a lack of understanding of the term angle of incidence.
>
> **(h)** The practical reasons why the results obtained may not be exactly those that the theory of reflection predicts were not well known. The main difficulty, which was not clear to most candidates, was the difficulty in lining up the pins P3 and P4 with the images of P1 and P2 so that
>
> they all appeared exactly one behind the other. Answers such as suggesting that the pins were too thick or that the lines drawn to represent the rays were too thick were also accepted.
