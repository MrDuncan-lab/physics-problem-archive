# Question 9 (7 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2022 · Paper 4 — Theory (Extended), вариант 3 · вопрос 9  
Источник: [[_pdf/2022/0625_s22_qp_43.pdf#page=12|условие, стр. 12–13]] · [[_pdf/2022/0625_s22_ms_43.pdf#page=10|схема оценивания, стр. 10]] · [[_pdf/2022/0625_s22_er.pdf#page=33|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Описание индукционного тока при колебаниях магнита и расчёт числа витков повышающего трансформатора.

**(a)** Fig. 9.1 shows a magnet on the end of a spring and a coil of wire connected to a sensitive centre-zero galvanometer. The magnet can move freely through the coil.

![[0625-s22-43-p12-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 9.1**

**(i)** The magnet is pulled down and released.

Describe and explain what happens to the needle of the sensitive galvanometer. **[4]**

**(ii)** The magnet is replaced with a stronger magnet.

State the effect of using a stronger magnet on what happens to the needle of the galvanometer. **[1]**

**(b)** A step-up transformer is used to step up the output voltage of a power station from 25 000 V to 400 000 V for transmission along power lines.

The number of turns on the secondary coil is 36 000.

Calculate the number of turns on the primary coil.

number of turns =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)** 2300

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | any **four** from:<br>• needle oscillates (as magnet moves up and down)<br>• coil cuts magnetic field / magnetic field changes (as magnet moves)<br>• <u>changing (magnetic) field induces voltage/current</u><br>• induced voltage/current opposes the motion/change causing it<br>• force, magnetic field and induced current are mutually perpendicular | B4 |
> | (a)(ii) | larger (maximum) deflection | B1 |
> | (b) | 2300 | A2 |
> |  | (*N*$_{\text{P}}$ =) *V*$_{\text{P}}$ *N*$_{\text{S}}$ / *V*$_{\text{S}}$ in any form OR (*N*$_{\text{P}}$ =) $\dfrac{25000 \times 36000}{400000}$ | C1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a) (i)** Stronger candidates realised that the motion of the magnet in the coil is an example of electromagnetic induction and causes a current to flow in the galvanometer. Knowledge that the direction of induced current is reversed when the direction of motion of the magnet is reversed allowed candidates to identify a galvanometer needle which will oscillate as the magnet oscillates up and down. There was confusion in some answers that suggested candidates may have thought that the needle was part of a small plotting compass and that, as shown on the diagram, it was pointing upwards because that is the direction of the North Pole.
>
> **(ii)** Those candidates who identified electromagnetic induction in **(i)** were often able to state that the needle gives a larger deflection. Common incorrect answers included faster deflection or the idea that the galvanometer needle was more sensitive. Neither of these described a bigger deflection and so were not given credit.
>
> **(b)** Most candidates who were able to recall the equation Np/Ns = Vp/Vs were also able to rearrange it correctly, substitute in the data and calculate the correct number of turns on the primary coil. Weaker candidates often wrote the original equation down incorrectly and so gained no credit. Candidates should be encouraged to write down any formula they intend to use in the learned format first before attempting a rearrangement of the terms as they may be awarded partial credit for this.
