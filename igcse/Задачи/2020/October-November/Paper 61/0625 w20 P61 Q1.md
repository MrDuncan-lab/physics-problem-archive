A student determines the density of modelling clay by two methods.

**Method 1**

*l* =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ cm

*w* =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ cm **[1]**

**(ii)** Calculate the volume *V*$_{\text{A}}$ of sample A using the equation *V*$_{\text{A}}$ = *l* × *w* × *d*.

*V*$_{\text{A}}$ =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ cm$^{3}$ **[1]**

**(iii)** Fig. 1.2 shows sample A on a balance. Record the mass *m*$_{\text{A}}$ of sample A to the nearest g.

**(iv)** Calculate the density ρ$_{\text{A}}$ of sample A of modelling clay using the equation ρ$_{\text{A}}$ = $\dfrac{m_{A}}{V}_{\text{A}}$.

Give your answer to a suitable number of significant figures for this method and include the unit.

ρ$_{\text{A}}$ =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

150

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a) (i)–(iv)** The majority of candidates successfully recorded the *l* and *w* values in cm. The volume calculation
>
> was correct in most answers. Most candidates gave the mass to the nearest g. The density needed
>
> to be given correctly and with the unit g / cm$^{3}$. A few candidates tried to convert to kg / m$^{3}$ which would have been acceptable but most who attempted the conversion did so incorrectly.
>
> **(b) (i)** Most candidates recorded the volume correctly.
>
> **(ii)** Candidates were expected to give the value of density to two or three significant figures.
>
> **(c)** Here candidates were required to make a judgement based on their own answers. The justification needed to be clear and consistent with the results to score credit. Most candidates scored partial credit, but many made a vague comment without showing any understanding of the concept of results being beyond the limits of experimental inaccuracy.
>
> **(d)** Candidates were expected to know the best way to read a value from a measuring cylinder but significant numbers of candidates did not tick the appropriate boxes “perpendicular to the scale” and “in line with the bottom of the meniscus”.
