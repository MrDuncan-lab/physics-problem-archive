**(b)** A student attaches a liquid-in-glass thermometer to a ruler. The thermometer does **not** have a marked scale. Fig. 6.2 shows the arrangement.

![[0625-w20-31-p12-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 6.2**

The student places the thermometer in steam. The mercury rises to 11 cm on the ruler.

The student places the thermometer in melting ice. The mercury decreases to 1 cm on the ruler.

Determine the temperature indicated by the mercury level in Fig. 6.2.

temperature =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ °C **[3]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)** (temperature =) 50 (°C)

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | A: melting | B1 |
> |  | B: evaporating / boiling / vapourising | B1 |
> |  | C: freezing / solidifying | B1 |
> | (b) | 1(.0) cm = 10 °C OR (6(.0) – 1(.0)) = 5 | B1 |
> |  | $\dfrac{5 \times 100}{11 - 1}$ | C1 |
> |  | (temperature =) 50 (°C) | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** The vast majority of candidates scored full credit as the simple kinetic molecular model of matter was well understood.
>
> **(b)** The majority of candidates answered this fairly well. The most common error was in using the limits of the thermometer rather than the information given in the question in attempting to calculate the temperature indicated by the thermometer.
