# Question 2 (7 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · February/March 2021 · Paper 3 — Theory (Core), вариант 2 · вопрос 2  
Источник: [[_pdf/2021/0625_m21_qp_32.pdf#page=4|условие, стр. 4]] · [[_pdf/2021/0625_m21_ms_32.pdf#page=5|схема оценивания, стр. 5]] · [[_pdf/2021/0625_m21_er.pdf#page=5|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

Темы: [[1.4 Density]], [[1.3 Mass and weight]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Определить объём и плотность камня, объяснить различие массы и веса.

Fig. 2.1 shows a measuring cylinder containing water.

Fig. 2.2 shows the same measuring cylinder after a stone has been lowered into it.

![[0625-m21-32-p4-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 2.1 Fig. 2.2**

**(a)** Calculate the volume of the stone.

volume =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ cm$^{3}$ **[2]**

**(b)** Another stone has a mass of 98.4 g. The volume of this stone is 41.0 cm$^{3}$.

Calculate the density of the stone.

density =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ g / cm$^{3}$ **[3]**

**(c)** The stone with a mass of 98.4 g has a weight of 0.984 N. Explain the difference between mass and weight. **[2]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(a)** 24 (cm$^{3}$)
>
> **(b)** 2.4(0) (g / cm$^{3}$)

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | (volume =) difference in candidate’s readings | C1 |
> |  | 24 (cm$^{3}$) | A1 |
> | (b) | (density =) mass ÷ volume | C1 |
> |  | (density =) 98.4 ÷ 41.0 | C1 |
> |  | 2.4(0) (g / cm$^{3}$) | A1 |
> | (c) | idea that mass is (a measure of) amount of matter in a body | B1 |
> |  | idea that weight is a gravitational force | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Almost all candidates correctly calculated the volume of the stone as 24cm$^{3}$. However, weaker candidates attempted to read the volume nearest to the top of the stone and gave the answer of 30cm$^{3}$.
>
> **(b)** The majority of candidates were able to correctly calculate the density of the stone as 2.4 g / cm$^{3}$.
>
> **(c)** Most candidates gained at least partial credit for this question. Many candidates gave clear and precise descriptions of differences between mass and weight.
