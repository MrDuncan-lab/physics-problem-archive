# Question 2 (7 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2021 · Paper 3 — Theory (Core), вариант 2 · вопрос 2

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Описать точное измерение толщины монеты, вычислить плотность и определить возможность плавания в ртути.

**(a)** A coin collector has 19 identical coins, as shown in Fig. 2.1.

![[0625-w21-32-p3-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 2.1**

Fig. 2.2 shows one of the coins in the coin collector’s hand.

![[0625-w21-32-p3-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 2.2**

The coin collector wants to check the thickness of one coin. She has a 30 cm ruler.

Describe how she can use the 30 cm ruler to determine the thickness of one coin accurately.

You may include a diagram if you wish. **[3]**

**(b)** The coin collector finds another coin. She thinks this coin is made of gold.

She performs an experiment to find the coin’s density.

She obtains the following results:

mass of coin = 52.5 g

volume of coin = 5.4 cm$^{3}$

**(i)** Show that the density of this coin is about 10 g / cm$^{3}$. **[3]**

**(ii)** The density of liquid mercury is 13.6 g / cm$^{3}$. State and explain whether the coin in **(b)(i)** floats on liquid mercury. **[1]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)(i)** 9.7(2) (g / cm$^{3}$)

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | any **three** from:<br>(put some coins) on top of each other OR in a stack idea<br>measure the (total) thickness (of stack)<br>10 or more coins<br>thickness (of one coin) = total thickness / ’length’ ÷ number of coins | B3 |
> | (b)(i) | (D) = M ÷ V in any form | C1 |
> |  | 52.5 ÷ 5.4 | C1 |
> |  | 9.7(2) (g / cm$^{3}$) | A1 |
> | (b)(ii) | floats AND coin is less dense (than mercury) ora | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** The recall of how to obtain an average value for a small length by measuring multiples was varied. Many candidates gave a fully correct answer, often with a clear labelled diagram to support the written answer. Others merely measured the thickness of one coin using the ruler and some repeated the measurement several times and found the average value.
>
> **(b) (i)** Most candidates answered this question well, writing the equation and showing full working. The question asked candidates to show that the density was about 10 g/cm$^{3}$. Therefore, the final value of 9.7(2) needed to be seen to gain full credit.
>
> **(ii)** The vast majority of candidates gave a correct explanation but a number of candidates did not state if the coin would float or not.
