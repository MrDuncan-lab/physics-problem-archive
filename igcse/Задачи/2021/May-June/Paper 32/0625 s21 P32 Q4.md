# Question 4 (6 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2021 · Paper 3 — Theory (Core), вариант 2 · вопрос 4  
Источник: [[_pdf/2021/0625_s21_qp_32.pdf#page=5|условие, стр. 5]] · [[_pdf/2021/0625_s21_ms_32.pdf#page=6|схема оценивания, стр. 6]] · [[_pdf/2021/0625_s21_er.pdf#page=19|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 s21 P32]]  
Темы: [[1.7.1 Energy]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Описать превращения энергии катящегося шара и объяснить уменьшение высоты подъёма потерями энергии.

A student investigates energy changes when a ball rolls down a curved track.

The student holds the ball at a starting point on the curved track, as shown in Fig. 4.1.

![[0625-s21-32-p5-f1.png]]

**Fig. 4.1**

The ball is released. It rolls down the track, up the other side to the stopping point and then back down again.

**(a)** Describe the energy changes that take place as the ball rolls from the starting point to the stopping point. **[4]**

**(b)** The height of the stopping point is less than the height of the starting point.

Describe how the principle of conservation of energy explains the difference between the height of the stopping point and the height of the starting point. **[2]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | any **four** from:<br>• (gravitational) potential energy (of ball)<br>• (is) transformed/transferred to OR becomes kinetic energy (of ball)<br>• <u>as it falls or moves down slope must be linked to energy (change)</u><br>• kinetic energy transferred to (gravitational) potential energy<br>• <u>(as) ball rises or moves up slope must be linked to energy (change)</u><br>• (transferred to) thermal energy (due to friction) | B4 |
> | (b) | any **two** from:<br>• some (of the initial) (G)PE/energy is transferred<br>• (to) thermal energy<br>• (due to) friction/air resistance<br>• ball has less (G)PE/energy at stopping point | B2 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Many candidates gained partial credit, but only the strongest candidates gained full credit. The most common errors involved failing to identify the transfers of energy and linking them to different parts of the motion.
>
> **(b)** The majority of candidates identified that some of the energy had been transferred but failed to state the form to which it was transferred or failed to identify the mechanism of transfer.
