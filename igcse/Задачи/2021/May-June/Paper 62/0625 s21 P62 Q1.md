> Определить объём сосуда и плотность песка по измерениям размеров и массы с учётом точности отсчётов.

A student determines the density of sand.

Fig. 1.1 shows a beaker with a mark at the 250 cm$^{3}$ level.

![[0625-s21-62-p2-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 1.1**

**(a)** Estimate the volume of water *V*$_{\text{W}}$ that the beaker would hold when filled to the top.

*V*$_{\text{W}}$ =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ cm$^{3}$ **[1]**

**(b)** The student uses string and a metre rule to determine the circumference *c* of the beaker.

21.3 cm

*c* =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

Explain briefly how to use the string and the metre rule to determine the circumference *c* as accurately as possible. You may draw a diagram. **[2]**

**(c)** The student measures the height *h* of the beaker.

**(i)** Show clearly on Fig. 1.1, the height *h* that he should measure. **[1]**

9.0 cm

His reading is *h* =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(ii)** Calculate the external volume *V*$_{\text{B}}$ of the beaker using the equation

*V*$_{\text{B}}$ = $\dfrac{hc}{12.6}^{2}$ .

*V*$_{\text{B}}$ =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ cm$^{3}$ **[2]**

**(d)** The student measures the mass of the beaker on a balance, as shown in Fig. 1.2.

**(i)** Write down the mass *m*$_{\text{B}}$ of the beaker, to the nearest gram.

![[0625-s21-62-p3-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 1.2**

*m*$_{\text{B}}$ =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ g **[1]**

The student fills the beaker to the top with dry sand. He measures the mass *m* of the beaker containing the sand.

724 g

*m* =  \_\_\_\_\_\_

**(ii)** Calculate the mass *m*$_{\text{S}}$ of sand in the beaker. Use the equation *m*$_{\text{S}}$ = (*m* – *m*$_{\text{B}}$).

*m*$_{\text{S}}$ =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ g **[1]**

**(iii)** Calculate the density ρ of the sand using the equation

ρ = $\dfrac{m_{S}}{V}_{\text{B}}$ .

Include the unit.

ρ =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[2]**

**(e)** The student uses a measuring cylinder to measure the volume of dry sand. Draw a diagram of the measuring cylinder and show the line of sight that the student must use to obtain an accurate volume reading. **[1]**

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Many candidates gave a sensible estimate of the volume of water that the beaker could hold when filled to the top. Overestimates were relatively rare. Most incorrect answers were underestimates, with many candidates quoting values of 250 cm$^{3}$ or less. Occasionally, an answer within the allowed range could not be credited because the candidate was too precise and quoted the answer to the nearest cm$^{3}$, or even less.
>
> **(b)** Most candidates gave clear and concise accounts of how they would use the string and ruler to determine an accurate value of the circumference of the beaker. Of those candidates who wrapped the string around the beaker multiple times, a few forgot to make a suitable division. Another acceptable method used by a much smaller percentage of candidates was to place the string across the diameter of the beaker and divide their measured length by pi. Candidates who followed the suggestion, and produced a well-drawn diagram, gained credit from the diagram alone.
>
> **(c)** Many candidates did not give an answer for this question. However, most calculations for the external volume of the beaker were correct, but many candidates then quoted their answer to too many significant figures.
>
> **(d)** Some candidates seemed unfamiliar with the expression “to the nearest gram” and wrote down the mass of the beaker as 208.3 g. The subtraction to calculate the mass of sand in the beaker was almost always correct. The density calculation was usually correct, but occasionally an incorrect unit was provided. Common incorrect units seen were cm$^{3}$ / g, Pa and M / V.
>
> **(e)** The correct line of sight for an accurate reading of the volume of sand in the measuring cylinder was drawn by the majority of candidates. However, some candidates had drawn nothing in the measuring cylinder or the perpendicular viewing was not indicated.
