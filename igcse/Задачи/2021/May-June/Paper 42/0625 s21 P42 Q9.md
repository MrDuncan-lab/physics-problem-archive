# Question 9 (5 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · May/June 2021 · Paper 4 — Theory (Extended), вариант 2 · вопрос 9  
Источник: [[_pdf/2021/0625_s21_qp_42.pdf#page=13|условие, стр. 13]] · [[_pdf/2021/0625_s21_ms_42.pdf#page=10|схема оценивания, стр. 10]] · [[_pdf/2021/0625_s21_er.pdf#page=27|отчёт экзаменаторов]]  
Работа: [[0625 s21 P42]]  
Темы: [[4.2.2 Electric current]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Сопоставить направления электронного и условного тока и вычислить число электронов, проходящих за секунду.

**(a)** Fig. 9.1 shows a circuit.

![[0625-s21-42-p13-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 9.1**

On Fig. 9.1, draw **two** clearly labelled arrows to show the direction of the electron flow and the direction of the conventional current in the circuit. **[2]**

**(b)** The current in the motor is 13 A. The charge on an electron is 1.6 × 10$^{\text{–19}}$ C.

Calculate the number of electrons that pass through the motor every second.

number of electrons =  \_\_\_\_\_\_ **[3]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)** (n = 13 / 1.6 × 10$^{\text{–19}}$ =) 8.1 × 10$^{19}$

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a) | anti-clockwise arrow labelled (conventional) current somewhere in circuit | B1 |
> |  | electron (flow) arrow opposite to (conventional) current | B1 |
> | (b) | Q = It in any form or (Q =) It OR 13 × 1 | C1 |
> |  | (Q = It =) 13 × 1 (= 13 C) | C1 |
> |  | (n = 13 / 1.6 × 10$^{\text{–19}}$ =) 8.1 × 10$^{19}$ | A1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** Almost all candidates gave the correct directions for the current and flow of electrons. A few gave both in the reverse direction but still gained partial credit. The most common error was drawing unlabelled arrows that could not be given credit.
>
> **(b)** Many candidates gave the correct answer. Many who did not, gained partial credit from correctly quoting *Q* = *It*. The most common error for those who gave *Q* = *It*, but then did not go on to get the correct answer was in not substituting the numbers correctly and consequently dividing the charge on the electron by the current.
>
> Many candidates used incorrect symbols such as *C* for charge or for current which could not be credited.
