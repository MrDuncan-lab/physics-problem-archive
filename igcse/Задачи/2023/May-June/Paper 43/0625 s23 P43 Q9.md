> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a)** This question was very well answered and full credit could be awarded for Saturn and Jupiter placed correctly on the diagram. Partial credit was available for weaker candidates who calculated correct angles based on the ratio concept where the time of orbit represents 360°.
>
> **(b) (i)** The same technique as in **(a)** was required to answer this question. Stronger candidates realised that their calculation of 600° angular displacement of Jupiter equated to a full rotation plus a further 240° and this placed both planets 240° anticlockwise measured from S0.
>
> **(ii)** This question was answered well. Weaker candidates thought the question was testing knowledge of the Earth’s orbit around the Sun, observing that it was in the same place after 20 years.
>
> **(c) (i)** The vast majority of candidates answered this question correctly.
>
> **(ii)** When discussing density, most candidates found it straightforward to relate Jupiter’s low density to its gaseous composition. When discussing the gravitational field strength, stronger candidates were able to relate Jupiter’s large mass to its large value of gravitational field strength but weaker candidates just associated Jupiter’s large size with its large gravitational field strength.
>
> **(d)** The majority of candidates were able to correctly quote the density equation and then multiply density by volume. It proved difficult for most candidates to calculate the correct mass because they were unable to convert km$^{3}$ to m$^{3}$ where a multiplication factor of (1000)$^{3}$ was required. Candidates are advised to refer to the mathematical requirements of the syllabus as they should be able to convert between metric units.
