> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Измерить удлинение пружины при разных нагрузках, построить график и определить промежуточное удлинение.

In this experiment, you will investigate the stretching of a spring.

Carry out the following instructions, referring to Fig. 1.1.

![[0625-s23-52-p2-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 1.1**

**(a)** The metre ruler is clamped in position near to the spring. Do **not** change the position of the metre ruler.

**(i)** The value *l*$_{0}$ is the length of the spring when the load *L* is 0.0 N.

Measure the unstretched length *l*$_{0}$ of the spring. Do **not** include the loops at the ends of the spring in your measurement.

Record *l*$_{0}$ in cm to the nearest mm in Table 1.1. **[1]**

**(ii)** Draw a diagram of the spring to show clearly the length *l*$_{0}$ of the spring. **[1]**

- Suspend a load *L* = 1.0 N from the spring.
- Record the new length *l* of the spring in Table 1.1.
- Calculate the extension *e* of the spring using the equation *e* = (*l* – *l*$_{0}$).
- Record the value of *e* in Table 1.1.
- Repeat the procedure using loads *L* = 2.0 N, 3.0 N, 4.0 N and 5.0 N.
- Record all the readings and results in Table 1.1.

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a) (i)** Most candidates measured and recorded the unstretched length of the spring and also recorded it in the appropriate place in the table. The instruction to record the length in centimetres to the nearest millimetre was usually followed. However, sometimes the length of the spring was recorded to the nearest centimetre and sometimes, even to 0.01 cm.
>
> **(ii)** Candidates were then required to draw a diagram of the spring to clearly show the length that they had measured. There was much evidence to suggest that many candidates did not read the question carefully. These candidates tried to show how to measure the length, drawing rulers and set squares, rather than just doing as asked, and showing the length that they had measured, e.g. with a line or an arrow on a sketch of the spring. Many candidates spent time drawing the detailed set-up but then failed to identify the actual length that needed to be measured.
>
> **(b)** The actual experiment was performed well, with most candidates completing the table to show the length of the spring as the load on the spring was varied.
>
> The calculation of the extensions produced by the loads was done well, but a number of candidates calculated incremental, rather than total extensions. Some candidates rounded their correctly calculated extensions to the nearest whole number. This was presumably to make the graph plotting exercise which followed easier. This is not good practice and candidates should be discouraged from doing this.
>
> **(c)** Graph-plotting skills were of a reasonable standard, but many responses failed to gain full credit. The most common sources of error were:
>
> - awkward scales, such as 3 or 7; choosing such scales makes the points much harder to plot
>
> - missing labels and/or units on the axes
>
> - choosing scales so that the plotted points did not cover at least half of the given grid
>
> - failure to plot the first point for which *L* = 0 and *e* = 0
>
> - forcing the line of best fit to pass through the origin (whether plotted or not), resulting in an inappropriate line of best fit.
>
> There were, however, still many excellent, carefully drawn, best-fit lines produced by candidates.
>
> **(d)** Although most candidates gained full credit for this question, some candidates did not. Candidates were asked to indicate on the graph, how they obtained the information. This was often missing, as was the unit of their answer.
