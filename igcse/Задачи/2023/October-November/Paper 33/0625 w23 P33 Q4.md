# Question 4 (8 marks)

**IGCSE 0625 Physics** · October/November 2023 · Paper 3 — Theory (Core), вариант 3 · вопрос 4  
Источник: [[_pdf/2023/0625_w23_qp_33.pdf#page=7|условие, стр. 7]] · [[_pdf/2023/0625_w23_ms_33.pdf#page=9|схема оценивания, стр. 9]] · [[_pdf/2023/0625_w23_er.pdf#page=20|отчёт экзаменаторов]]

Темы: [[2.3.1 Conduction]], [[2.3.2 Convection]], [[2.3.3 Radiation]]

> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Объяснить выбор материалов и поверхности кастрюли и конвекционный нагрев комнаты.

**(a)** Fig. 4.1 shows a pan on a hotplate. The hotplate heats the pan and the water.

![[0625-w23-33-p7-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 4.1**

Use your ideas about thermal energy transfer to explain why the pan has:

**(i)** a wooden handle **[1]**

**(ii)** shiny sides **[2]**

**(iii)** a copper base. **[1]**

**(b)** Fig. 4.2 shows a heater for warming a room. When there is hot water in the heater, thermal energy transfers from the water to the room.

![[0625-w23-33-p7-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 4.2**

Explain how thermal energy from the heater warms the entire room. Use your ideas about the density of air. You may draw on Fig. 4.2. **[4]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | reduces heat transfer (to handle) OR wood is an insulator | B1 |
> | (a)(ii) | poor / worst emitter (of heat) | B1 |
> |  | (of) infrared / radiation | B1 |
> | (a)(iii) | allows heat transfer (to water) OR (good) conductor (of heat) | B1 |
> | (b) | any **four** from:<br>conduction (through metal of heater)<br>air particles gain kinetic energy / KE<br>air particles move further apart<br>hot air becomes less dense<br>hot / less dense air rises OR cold / denser air falls<br>idea of convection current | B4 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> **(a) (i)** The vast majority of candidates gave the correct answer.
>
> **(ii)** Very few candidates recognised that a shiny surface is a poor emitter of radiation.
>
> **(iii)** The vast majority of candidates gave the correct answer.
>
> **(b)** Candidates needed a fuller understanding of how density changes explain the formation of convection currents in air. A number of candidates gained partial credit for recognising that a convection current would be formed and/or that the heated air would rise.
