> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Измерение напряжения на участках провода и падения напряжения на единицу длины.

*In this experiment, you will investigate the voltage across a wire.*

Record all of your observations and answers on page 8 of your Answer Booklet.

Carry out the following instructions referring to Fig 4.1, which shows the circuit that has been set up for you.

![[0625-w03-5-p5-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 4.1**

**(a)** Place the sliding contact **C** on the resistance wire **AB** at a distance *x* from **A** where *x* = 0.200 m.

**(b)** Record the value of *x*.

**(c)** Switch on and, using the voltmeter, record the voltage *V* across **AC**.

**(d)** Take the sliding contact away from the wire **AB** and switch off.

**(e)** Calculate the voltage *k* per unit length of the wire using the equation

*k* = $\dfrac{V}{x}$ .

**(f)** Repeat steps **(b)** – **(e)** with the sliding contact at distances of *x* = 0.400 m and *x* = 0.800 m from **A**.

**(g)** Complete the column headings in the table.

**(h)** Within the limits of experimental error, what do you conclude about the variation of voltage with distance along the wire? Justify your conclusion by reference to your table of results.

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (b)–(g) The majority of candidates recorded the lengths and voltages correctly with x values given to 3 dp
>
> in metres and V values given to at least 1 dp in volts. Full marks for k were awarded if the values were arithmetically correct, all to 2 or all to 3 sf, the unit V/m and all the values the same to within ten percent. Many candidates scored most of the available marks. The most common cause of a lost mark was inconsistency in the number of significant figures to which the k value was recorded.
>
> (h) It was more difficult for candidates to look at their results and write a conclusion. Many candidates were able to record that as the length increased, the voltage increased. The best candidates were able to conclude that the voltage was proportional to the length and to justify that by writing that k is constant (within the limits of experimental error).
