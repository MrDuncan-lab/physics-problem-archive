> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Положение центра масс и устойчивость предметов мебели и лабораторной стойки.

**(a)** The block of wood in Fig. 1.1 will balance on a horizontal table.

![[0625-w03-2-p2-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 1.1**

If the block is tilted slightly clockwise about AB, it returns to its original position when released.

**(i)** On Fig. 1.1, mark with the letter G where the centre of mass of the block might be.

**(ii)** Small masses are added to the top PQRS of the block until it is just about to topple (fall over). Fig. 1.2 shows a side view of the block.

![[0625-w03-2-p2-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 1.2**

On Fig. 1.2, draw a line along which the centre of mass of the arrangement must now lie. **[2]**

**(b)** Fig. 1.3 shows two ways of using a laboratory retort stand.

![[0625-w03-2-p3-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 1.3**

Which diagram shows the safer arrangement?  \_\_\_\_\_\_

Give a reason for your choice. **[2]**

**(c)** Some office filing cabinets have a mechanism that allows only one drawer to be opened at a time, as shown in Fig. 1.4.

![[0625-w03-2-p3-f2.svg]]

**Fig. 1.4**

Suggest why they have this mechanism. **[1]**

> [!answer]- Ответ (итоговые ответы из схемы оценивания)
> **(b)** more stable (or equivalent statement) e.g. less likely to topple or “weight within base”

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> There were clearly some cases where stability and centres of gravity could have received more study, because part (a) was poorly done by many candidates. Many drew diagonals and marked G at the intersection, and some put G on AB or SR, but very few had a genuine idea of where it should be. Anywhere to the left of the “vertical” through the centre of AB would have been acceptable for the mark, but few got this. There were similar problems with Fig. 1.2. However, (b) and (c) brought some good responses, most knowing the correct reasons. In (b) some weaker candidates thought that A was safer (correct), but that the reason was that the flask did not have as far to fall if it should slip.
