> [!abstract] Кратко (составлено моделью)
> Работа термопары и влияние поверхности на поглощение излучения.

Fig. 5.1 shows a thermocouple set up to measure the temperature at a point on a solar panel.

![[0625-s03-3-p6-f1.svg]]

**Fig. 5.1**

**(a)** X is a copper wire.

**(i)** Suggest a material for Y.

**(ii)** Name the component Z. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how a thermocouple is used to measure temperature. **[3]**

**(c)** Experiment shows that the temperature of the surface depends upon the type of surface used.

Describe the nature of the surface that will cause the temperature to rise most. **[1]**

> [!grading]- Схема оценивания (mark scheme)
> | Пункт | Ответ | Баллы |
> |---|---|---|
> | (a)(i) | Y is a wire of different metal/not copper | B1 |
> | (a)(ii) | Z is a galvanometer/millivoltmeter/milliammeter | B1 |
> | (b) | 2 junctions at different temperatures, accept one hot, one cold — B1<br>temperature difference causes e.m.f./voltage/current — B1<br>reading of meter changes (with temperature) — B1<br>1 junction at known temperature/need for calibration — B1 | max 3 |
> | (c) | dull or black surface | B1 |

> [!report]- Отчёт экзаменаторов
> (a) A poor level of correct response to both parts. In (i) even good candidates gave copper as the answer. In (ii) a wide variety of wrong or unacceptable suggestions included thermocouple, meter and readout. Any named meter was accepted provided it was capable of detecting an e.m.f. of millivolts or less or a current of milliamps or less.
>
> (b) Whilst some good answers were seen the vast majority had no idea. Only simple explanations were required in terms of the difference in junction temperatures producing an e.m.f. which was measured by a millivoltmeter which in turn could be calibrated to read in °C.
>
> (c) Most candidates stated a dull black surface. A good conductor of heat was a popular wrong answer.
